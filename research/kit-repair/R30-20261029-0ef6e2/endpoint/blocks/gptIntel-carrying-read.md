## The weighted proof

- Write `c(t)=f(t)−g(t)`, `w(t)=len(c(t))`, and `T(n)=Σ_{t<n}w(t)`. At a failed scale the first line copies throughout `1 ≤ t < r+d`. Distinct failures of the second line have distance at least `r+d`. This proves `Scale.copy` on that half-open range except at m, in both orientations. Summing on `[m+1,e)` proves `shift_one`, including the empty interval at `e=m+1` and the final legal endpoint `e=r+d`. If `m+d≤r`, the disjoint intervals `[1,m)` and `[d+1,d+m)` have equal weight. Adding the second interval to the full prefix `[0,m)` gives `T(r)≥2T(m)−w(0)`. The declared loss M covers the unrestricted index zero. [Weighted lemmas](Rule30RotationBlocks.lean)
- Fix the requested starting index H first. Failure of the first uniform slack selects a lag threshold. Beyond it both the no-failure case and `2T(m)≤T(r)+M` would already give the required repeat. Thus each late scale fails. At two successive scales `m′=m+d` by carry, so the failure at the third excludes `k=0` in `r′=d+k(r+d)`. The next-scale inequality `r′<m′+d′` excludes `k≥2`. This leaves `r′=r+2d`, with `m<r`. Two applications of `shift_one` give the signed change in `T(r)−2T(m)` as `2(w(m+d)−w(m))`. Successive failures toggle the value of c, which has only two possibilities at positive indices. This cancels that change after two scales, including when the lengths happen to coincide. The induction advances two scales, preserves the same S chosen at the initial late scale, and makes d arbitrarily large. Its final slack `S+2M+2MH` is fixed before the requested lag N. [Induction in arc_long_squares](Rule30RotationBlocks.lean)
- For block offset q, `blocks_excluded` requests a letter start at least `q+H`. Subtracting q from both indices yields actual natural-number indices. The prefix-length sums shift by `T(q)`, and positive block lengths preserve the lower bounds on start and lag. For every required bit `s+K<j′`, `locate` places s strictly inside the matched interval at an offset y within its block, including `y=0`. Equal earlier block lengths transfer the same y to the second interval, where the two Boolean outputs agree. The existing `no_near_squares` then moves the row until its leftmost black cell is strictly west of the observed column and applies `corollary_F`. - The real bridge tests membership in `[0,γ)` for `0<γ<1`. At the left endpoint it tests a floor difference of −1 with shift −γ, and at the right endpoint it tests +1 with shift +γ. The strict upper endpoint gives the correct value on a boundary hit. For integer translate d it discards `k=|d|` **blocks**, uses nonnegative line index `q=k+d`, and moves the physical clock by twice the **sum of those k block lengths**. This preserves the white phase for negative, zero and positive d. A tail starting inside a block can drop the finite remainder and begin at the next whole block. If the centre is white and its right neighbour black, that neighbour is still black one tick later. If the centre has meanwhile turned black, the next update of the neighbour is `1 XOR 1 = 0`. The proof of the golden exclusion uses this lemma and arithmetic on the word.
