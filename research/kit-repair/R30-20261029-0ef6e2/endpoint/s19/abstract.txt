Turn a circle by a fixed irrational angle and note at each step whether the point is in a fixed arc, starting on the orbit of an end of the arc. Take a row of Rule 30 with a leftmost black cell, and a column of it that alternates from some tick on. Then the column to its right, read at the white ticks, is not this word from any tick on, for any irrational angle and any arc, nor any block code of it (`rotation_block_excluded`, over the real numbers; `arc_excluded`, `no_eventual_arc_neighbour`, in integers). Half-circles (`half_circle_excluded`) and the characteristic Sturmian words of every irrational slope, from any index on (`sturmian_excluded`), are cases; the square roots are explicit (`sqrt_line`, `silver_excluded`). The reason concerns rotations alone: in every such word the stretch from some place i comes again from a later place j and agrees for j letters less a fixed slack, with j - i as large as wanted (`arc_near_squares`); for half-circles the slack is two (`half_circle_near_squares`). `corollary_F` of `Rule30ClockNearSquares` forbids this beside a clock. The proof is core Lean, by Farey neighbours in integers, with no continued fraction; `irrationalLine_iff` and `phaseLine_iff` say what its integer hypotheses mean for real lines, and need Mathlib. Our golden half-circle theorem has one angle and every phase. Free starting phases, and three independent cut orbits, stay open; nothing here excludes an alternating centre.
