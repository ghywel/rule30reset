# When can the second white tick carry information?

A gap of length q means one black pulse followed by q white ticks. At the second white tick, sample the cell immediately to the right of the driven column. This is the only white phase on which the established sparse-clock theorem leaves the late right-neighbor reading undetermined. The following tests identify which pulses can carry that variation. [Prior phase theorem](sparse-word-growth.md) · [AIOvernight’s merged reset theorem](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/40017e8/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30ResetMixtures.lean).

## An exact age on ordinary trajectories

**From relative tick 25, a black second-white reading must follow a pulse whose preceding gap had exactly nine whites**, for every drive with gaps in 9 through 16 starting at a pulse. This applies at every site of every ordinary full-line configuration. It also applies when the gap lengths change indefinitely. The clock starts at the first prescribed pulse, which may occur at any absolute time.

The version in gap counts has no upper bound on the gap lengths. After three completed gaps, a black second-white reading requires the last of those gaps to be nine. After just two completed gaps, the only exceptions with a longer final gap are (9,10) and (9,11). Both occur on actual finite rows, at ticks 23 and 24 respectively. For a single completed gap in the bounded alphabet the observation is at most 19 ticks from the start. These cases give exactly 25. With unbounded gaps a white right half just after the first black input can persist throughout an arbitrarily long first gap, so the gap-count and tick statements have different scopes.

The computation allows either right exterior bit at each tick. For minima (9,9,10), the width-eight sets after each gap contain 75, 69 and 38 rows. For (10,10) the sizes are 69 and 38. For (9,12) the width-nine sets have 118 and 61 rows. Each set includes its images under additional white ticks. The two-tick Rule 30 formula for row r at the next pulse is black exactly when bit 0 is white or bits 1 and 2 are both white. All three final sets give white. For sharpness, a separate evaluator constructs finite initial rows by determining one left cell per prescribed tick, then evolves those rows without any driven boundary. [Replay the three upper bounds and both physical witnesses](check-second-white.py).

On any proposed periodic sparse column in a configuration with a leftmost black cell, finite-left masked Jen forces each period compatible with the drive to fail again arbitrarily late in the sampled sequence. At least one of the two disagreeing readings is black. Thus the disagreement can always be found immediately downstream of a nine-white gap, including on the singleton if such a periodic tail exists. [Finite-left masked Jen](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/40017e8/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30FiniteMaskedJen.lean).

## How often two readings of the same input can disagree

Fix the initial and final right rows of the recurrent finite reader, and give two paths the same sequence of input gaps. At width 22, a discrepancy at a pulse requires the three immediately preceding gaps to be **(12,10,9)** and the gap starting there to be **10, 12 or 14**, wherever these letters lie inside the path. The statement includes wraparound for closed paths. The two paths may have different unobserved right completions at each gap. Consequently this is an upper bound for continuous ordinary histories with those same carried endpoints.

For each same-input row pair we check two conditions: it is reachable from an equal-row pair, and it can return to an equal-row pair. Their intersection is exactly the pairs that can occur on walks with shared endpoints. Follow equal labels backward three steps or forward one step from its different-color pairs to recover the context. A second search starts at all such discrepant pairs and asks how many positive steps it takes to reach another. An independent ordered-pair implementation repeats the context and shortest-distance tests at widths 22 and 24, allowing paths to revisit a pair at different lengths. These are exhaustively checked graph facts. Individual edges have physical witnesses, while a string of such edges may need incompatible exterior bits. [Ordinary reader and gluing test](ordinary-gaps.md).

For fixed input and endpoints at width 24, even the set of positions where *any* two readings can differ has spacing at least 12. If two coordinates could each vary, pick two outputs differing at the first coordinate. Either they differ at the second one too, or a third output differing there has to disagree with one of them at both coordinates. Thus every 12 consecutive samples admit at most two restrictions, and an n-gap window has at most **2^⌈n/12⌉** readings with fixed endpoints. The bound is on the output for a given drive. It does not assert positive entropy or existence of a physical infinite branch.

For a periodic input, record the input phase in the finite state and replace output at the specified context by a fixed value. Equal-input walks with common endpoints in any recurrent component now agree throughout. To check a discrepancy near the ends, prepend and append equal walks in the recurrent phase component. The finite no-diamond theorem therefore makes the masked output eventually periodic. Since the full second-white sequence must break eventual periodicity on a finite-left row, at least one of the indicated cyclic contexts must itself carry a non-eventually-periodic subsequence. This strengthens the earlier requirement that the three-gap word occur somewhere. [Finite no-diamond criterion and period tests](periodic-gap-requirements.md).
