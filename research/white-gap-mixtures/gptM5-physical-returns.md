# Physical returns of the compulsory nine-gap factor

**COMPUTED (R118–R121).** In the eventual sparse period of a Rule 30 column from a row with a leftmost black cell, two occurrences of the N12 factor must be at least **13 pulse intervals apart**. At a separation of exactly 13, the second-white sample at the later mark is always white. The two exclusions extend R117, which found a width-29 strip path at separation 11. [R117 and the nine-gap transfer](gptM5-n12-return-read.md).

An occurrence of the factor marks the pulse at the bar.

```text
a  12  14  12  12  10  9  |  10  b       a ∈ {10,14}, b ∈ {10,12}
```

At least one marked phase in the proposed period must have a non-eventually-periodic sample on successive turns, by N12. It must therefore be at least **14 pulse intervals after the preceding mark**, counting across the wrap. With m marked phases and p pulses in the period, this gives p ≥ 13m + 1. In particular a period with 18–26 pulses would have exactly one marked phase, which would have to carry the non-eventually-periodic sample.

## The eleven-pulse obstruction

Track all width-29 pulse rows that lie on some path reading both factors. At the fourth pulse interval after the first factor begins, the only 29-cell row is 515109925. The next pulse requires row 405982277. All compatible histories coming into the first row through the preceding 12-gap require its 30th cell to be black. To reach the next row through a second 12-gap, that same cell must be white.

The two 12-gaps give a 26-tick contradiction in one ordinary cone. The first of its three width-29 domains is {25205029, 377526565}, and the other two are the singletons above. Both LRAT checkers verified the resulting formula with 1,136 variables and 8,645 clauses. Two independent algorithms reproduced the prefix counts 172, 31, 11, 3, 1, 0 as soon as one more cell was carried. The pruned width-29 graph alone admits 2,591 final rows, with 570 possible pulse rows at the second mark and white in every case. [Carried-row checker](check-return-lift.cpp) · [Independent truth-table replay](check-return-obstruction.py) · [Fixed-cone check](certify-return-cones.py).

## One cone for all allowed gap words

At each pulse keep a prefix of length 29 plus the sum of the **maximum** remaining slot durations. Evolve it with one black driver tick and then whites. Select a legal gap, and copy the resulting prefix to the variables at the next pulse. Its length decreases by the maximum duration in that slot. This leaves enough cells even when the chosen gap is shorter. A physical history assigns those variables by taking its actual right prefixes. Conversely, evolve a satisfying initial prefix repeatedly using its chosen gaps. The resulting pulse rows and sample bits agree with the selected variables, since all their ancestors lie in the retained prefixes.

The width-29 membership constraints use the intersection of two Boolean row sets at each pulse. One contains rows reachable from the start, and the other contains rows from which the word can finish. This intersection is necessary for any physical path in the recurrent graph. The adjacency clauses enforce the exact one-gap relation of that graph, which every physical step between its rows obeys. The graph itself is the same one used in I217 and R117, with 353,356 rows and 3,326,068 edges. It is generated by exact one-gap images and recurrent pruning. A hypothetical finite-left sparse periodic column eventually follows it. Its sample must vary infinitely often by the sparse phase theorem and finite-left masked Jen, and S8 supplies internal 9 and 10 edges. Therefore the exclusion applies to each cyclic context by reading a sufficiently late occurrence. Every UNSAT in the table passed both `cake_lpr` and `lrat-check`. The distance-12 formula has 59,016 variables and 639,778 clauses. The distance-13 black-sample formula has 68,373 variables and 796,493 clauses. Literal cell-by-cell evolution reproduced all 23 recurrent pulse rows of the distance-13 witness over 276 ticks. Its four spacer gaps are 12, 9, 9, 14. Both factors start with 14 and end in 12. The right row has 209 black cells in the 305-cell initial cone. Kit realization extends the prescribed column and right half to an ordinary full-line row. The witness proves sharpness **for a finite history constrained to the recurrent graph**. An eventual finite-left sparse period realizing the separation remains open. The predictions that a carried 11-pulse path would survive, that any 12-pulse word would have a continuous recurrent history, and that a 13-pulse return could end with a black sample all failed. The surviving 13-pulse witness explains why excluding short returns of the factor and excluding short returns that transmit variation are distinct questions. The current result relies on the graph and external certificates. The next proof target is a small checked description of the pulse domains or a direct physical argument that the later sample is white at the shortest return. The earlier 7–9 direct capture remains the standalone Lean result. [Lean source](Rule30ShortMarkReturns.lean).

## Reproduction

The R121 bundle includes the graph, three complete all-words exclusion certificates, the two-gap certificate, the positive witnesses and the SHA-256 manifest. Use Python 3 with NumPy and SciPy. From this directory run the following with `G29` pointing at `g29.bin` and `OUT` at an existing output directory.

```sh
python3 check-return-obstruction.py "$G29" 11 > "$OUT/independent-11.json"
python3 check-return-obstruction.py "$G29" 12 > "$OUT/independent-12.json"
python3 certify-return-cones.py "$OUT" seam "$G29"
python3 check-all-physical-returns.py "$G29" "$OUT" 11
python3 check-all-physical-returns.py "$G29" "$OUT" 12
python3 check-all-physical-returns.py "$G29" "$OUT" 12 --control
python3 check-all-physical-returns.py "$G29" "$OUT" 13
python3 check-all-physical-returns.py "$G29" "$OUT" 13 --second-black
```
