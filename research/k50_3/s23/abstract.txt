In core Lean 4.35.0 we prove Hughes's two inverse-tick theorems for rule 30's left half driven by a wall column with arbitrary values (the kit's `Rule30DrivenLeft.L`), and find their least ages. The wall is at site 0 and follows any Boolean sequence. The start is any row whose leftmost black cell is at `-m`, white further left. From age 8 when `m ≥ 2`, and from age 9 when `m = 1`, every predecessor of the row at age `t` has its leftmost black cell at `-m - t + 1` (`Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.predecessor_edge`). From ages 24 and 25, every two-step predecessor has it at `-m - t + 2` (`Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.two_step_predecessor_edge`). The predecessors need satisfy rule 30 only on the negative sites, so their own wall bits are free. All four ages are the least (`Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.least_age_nonadjacent`, `Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.least_age_adjacent`, `Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.two_tick_least_age_nonadjacent`, `Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.two_tick_least_age_adjacent`), even for predecessors whose wall bit is the prescribed one (`Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.sharp_examples`, `Rule30Submission.Rule30DrivenLeftInverse.sharp_two_step_examples`). The proof reuses Hughes's good edge bands and the inverse reset of `Rule30TwoSidedInverse`. Narrow starts are settled by tables of 896 and 2,816 cases, computed by a packed update proved equal to the driven half. Beside the white wall these are Hughes's ages; next to the wall, some walls need one tick more. Nothing here concerns the centre column.
