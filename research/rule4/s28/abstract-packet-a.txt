In Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib we give the exact law of the right half of a fair Rule 30 row driven through a finite boundary history. Write μ for fair-coin measure. Prescribe the centre bits w(0) to w(T − 1), let F send the initial right half to the one at tick T, and let S be its range. For every measurable set E of right halves, μ(S ∩ E) = 2⁻ᵀ μ(F⁻¹E) (`Rule30Submission.RightHalfPast.after_exact`). So S is a closed set of mass 2⁻ᵀ, the output is fair measure conditioned on S (`Rule30Submission.RightHalfPast.mass_attainable`), distinct words have ranges that meet in a null set, and countably many infinite pasts are null. The identity holds again when a continuous rule chooses the whole boundary from the initial right half (`Rule30Submission.RightHalfFeedback.driven_exact`), in particular for the actual centre beside any fixed left half (`Rule30Submission.RightHalfFeedback.later_exact`). If, beside every finite left half with a black cell, each purely periodic centre word from tick zero has a null set of right halves, then beside each such left half the centre is almost surely not eventually periodic (`Rule30Submission.RightHalfFeedback.reduction`). The kit gives the independent fair trace and right half, fair time slices and the 010 predecessor window (`Rule30Equivalents`), but no law for the image of a driven half. The proof decodes the past from 010 windows far east, on one conull set valid against every comparison row, and integrates over the independent trace. The pure-word hypothesis is open, and nothing here bounds Rule 30's entropy from below.
