Watch one site of any Rule 30 history for T ticks. Let C count the ticks on which it is black, and A the ticks on which it is black and its right neighbour white. On every interval at every site, 3C ≤ T + 5A + 3, 2C ≤ T + 2A + 1 and 6C ≤ 5T + A + 5 (Rule30Submission.three_faces), and nothing more holds linearly: αC + βA ≤ γT + K holds for some K exactly when it holds with K = 0 at the five points (T, C, A) = (1,0,0), (3,1,0), (4,3,1), (10,9,4), (1,1,1) (Rule30Submission.linear_bound_iff), even over nonzero finite seeds. Five space-time periodic histories sit at these corners, and each corner is tracked with bounded error from an arbitrary right half. On the single seed the corners (3/4, 1/4) and (9/10, 2/5) are missed by a margin growing at least like log T, and bounded error in the middle inequality at a site is exactly eventual period two of the next column. The catalogue had the first inequality (Rule30SourceObservables: Condrey; Quint and Gemini) and, at the centre, the identity behind the second (Rule30TwoBlockCounts: Ulrey; Quint and Gemini). The third is new: by the kit's nine-black shield, a black run pays for at most five ticks with a black right neighbour. The proofs use bounded potentials on six cells, kernel-checked periodic histories and finite controllers steering through the left half. Nothing here bounds the density of the centre column: Problem 2 is untouched.
