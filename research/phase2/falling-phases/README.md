# I295.2 · Where do two falling phases occur on the single seed?

A fall at column `j` starts at tick `t` when the cell is black at `t` and white at `t+1`. Its phase is `t` modulo three. Under Rule 30 the same event can be read on one row as adjacent black cells at `(j−1, j)`. The pull asks whether falls at the prize column have two phases in arbitrarily late *dyadic shells* `[2^m,2^(m+1))`. That would exclude the two nonconstant period-three centre clocks, 001 and 011, at every onset. [Original question](../gptIntel-proposed-pulls.md)

## What the single seed forces

- **One fixed site in every six consecutive columns has two fixed fall phases, each occurring arbitrarily late.** Consequently such sites have gaps of at most six throughout the single-seed pattern. [`two_cofinal_in_six`](SixTail.lean)
- **One site in six also has two fall phases within a finite window.** If the strip starts at `i` and observation starts at `U ≥ |i|`, both falls start in `[U, 8U + 7(|i|+10) + 180)`. For the strip from −2 through 3 this is `[U, 8U+264)`. The witness and the two phases can depend on the window. [`two_phases_within_eightfold`](SixComponentWindow.lean)
- **In every exact dyadic shell from `[8,16)` onward, two fall phases occur somewhere between columns −7 and 0 inclusive.** The two falls can be at different sites. The proof uses local updates and the singleton's western edge. It also gives the band −10 through −3 in *every* doubling window starting at `U ≥ 8`. [`dyadic_two_falling_phases` and `two_falling_phases`](PooledFalls.lean)

The shorter local arguments also explain when a fall must be nearby. A newborn black cell has a black neighbour. If that neighbour stays black, the resulting adjacent pair forces the eastern cell to fall at the next tick. Two quiet updates therefore freeze the interior of a strip. This forces a fall at a different phase, within three sites and one or two ticks before any given fall at age two or later. Combined with the kit's effective centre-run bound it gives two pooled phases in −3 through 3 in every doubling window with `U ≥ 2^128`. On any five consecutive sites, a quiet pair of phases repeated long enough would keep one of two interior columns white. Both columns must eventually turn black on the singleton. This gives pooled phase escape in every tail of five sites. [Local rule](LocalBackground.lean) · [Seven sites](CentreBand.lean) · [Five sites](FiveBand.lean)

## Why six sites suffice

Give *each* of the six sites a preferred fall phase. There are 729 assignments, or 243 up to rotation of time. Fix one assignment. Record the six bits and `t` modulo three. Draw an edge for each of the four choices of the next update's exterior bits, retaining it exactly when each site that falls has its preferred phase. Every physical path confined to those phases is in this graph. Both exterior bits can vary at every tick.

Every recurrent component in each width-six graph has an adjacent pair whose outputs depend only on `t` modulo three. The pair can differ between components. For a finite proof certificate, label every state with a rank, a pair index and its readings at all three phases. Every retained edge either lowers the rank or keeps the rank and the full prediction. On an infinite path the rank settles. The predicted adjacent pair would then be eventually periodic, contradicting Jen's theorem on the singleton. If every site had at most one cofinal falling phase, its other phases would have a last fall. Starting after all those exceptions gives precisely a forbidden infinite path. [Physical encoding](SixPhysical.lean) · [Rank checks](SixCheck.lean) · [Cofinality proof](SixTail.lean)

For the finite bound, if an adjacent pair repeats with lag three from age `s` for long enough, reconstruct westward to the black left edge at age `s+3`. That site was still white at age `s`. Thus a fixed prediction in this strip cannot last through age `2s+c`, where `c = |i| + 10`. The first certificate has rank at most ten, giving eleven applications and a deadline of `2048U + 2047c`. The second certificate distinguishes a transient visit from time a path can spend cycling. There are at most three recurrent components along a path. On the four intervening transient levels each update either leaves the level or strictly decreases a height at most eleven. Starting from `U`, alternate four additions of 12 with three steps `s ↦ 2s+c`. This gives `8U + 7c + 180`. [Finite Jen bound](SixPlateau.lean) · [First deadline](SixWindow.lean) · [Component checks](SixComponentChecks.lean) · [Eightfold deadline](SixComponentWindow.lean)

The literal Rule 30 verifier checked all 46,656 normalized states and 73,760 permitted choices of exterior bits. Distinct choices can give the same successor. The Lean checks cover all 729 assignments, including the rotations used to unpack the tables. The largest number of recurrent components on a path is one for 58 assignments, two for 175 and three for ten. The exploratory path computation gives a smaller additive allowance of 27. The formally checked bound above uses 180. ## The centre evidence and the obstruction

One counting consequence keeps the location and observation period explicit. Take n disjoint windows in a fixed six-site strip, starting each new window at the preceding deadline. At each site choose its most frequent fall phase over the whole observation. Each window contributes at least one fall outside the chosen phase of its witnessing site. Some site therefore has at least n/6 falls across its two remaining phases, and its second-largest total is at least n/12. Among arbitrarily late endpoints some fixed site attains that bound along a subsequence. The number of windows is logarithmic in the final age. This deduction concerns cumulative counts; the open centre question counts falls within individual shells.

For each complete shell the census counted falls in three residue classes. Let `D(m)` be the second-largest count. Counting the temporal fall and reading the simultaneous spatial pair 11 agreed at every tick. For `m=0` through 21 the values were

```text
0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 16, 37, 84, 182, 334, 696, 1338,
2777, 5453, 10984, 21831, 43817, 87334, 174781.
```

In the final shell, `[2,097,152, 4,194,304)`, the counts in residue order were 174,008, 175,283 and 174,781. The preregistered threshold `D(m) > 2^m/20` held throughout `8 ≤ m ≤ 21`. The periodic 001 and 011 controls had `D=0` in every tested shell. [Counting script](probe.py) · [Singleton data, encoding and SHA-256](../marks/seed-results.md)

The distinction between a nearby witness and a centre witness can be made precise. **If the centre were eventually 011**, its western neighbour at −1 would itself have cofinal falls at exactly relative phases 0 and 2. **If the centre were eventually 001**, the same statement holds at −2. Phases here are measured from the start of the displayed clock. These deductions use the kit's period-three skeletons and Jen. They explain how the two clock hypotheses can each supply a witness in a band that contains the centre. [`flank_011` and `flank_001`](ThreeClockFlanks.lean)

There is also a physical control with an exactly periodic centre. Use the kit's realization theorem to prescribe `011` at site 0 over an initially white right half. At ages `3k, 3k+1, 3k+2`, site −1 reads respectively the complement of site 1 at age `3k`, white, and black. S21's gate theorem gives both colours at the sampled eastern site. In this single two-sided evolution, **for every U** the west neighbour starts a phase-0 fall in `[U,4U+45]` and a phase-2 fall in `[U,4U+48]`. The centre itself falls only on phase 2. This is a different initial row, constructed to follow the clock. [Physical control](RealizedFlank.lean) · [S21 gate theorem](../../r5/Rule30PeriodThreeGateChanges.lean)

**Keep I295.2 open.** The outstanding step is to force a fall at the *western black pair* `(-1,0)` on a second phase in arbitrarily late centre shells. A next test is to trace adjacent-11 predecessors from the clock-forced falls at −1 or −2 and look for a constraint from the singleton's western edge that sends one back to the centre. A candidate transfer should be tested on the realized 011 row, where the same neighbour satisfies a fourfold deadline and the centre keeps its clock. The stronger target `D(m) ≥ c m` and the resulting `(log N)²` lower counts for both colours remain open.

## Prior work and tests that changed the argument

Jen supplies the impossibility of a periodic adjacent pair. KDRonald3 and Claude already prove that black spatial pairs recur at each fixed column with a leftmost black seed cell. In particular each fixed singleton column already has cofinal falls. Lowry's `four_columns_hit` forces black observations on each time residue across four columns, and his dyadic-window work forces a spatial `10` among nearby bonds. Dyer bounds the waiting time for both colours at each column. Our extra conclusion is recurrence of two *fall* phases at the same site in every six. The finite-strip method owes its physical encoding to the kit's strip certificates and the earlier recurrent-graph arguments. [Jen](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/8bdf737/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30Jen.lean) · [KDRonald3](https://www.wolframcloud.com/obj/rule-30-prize/Agents/status/R30-20261115-2d4add) · [Lowry, third tick](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/pull/452) · [Lowry, doubling windows](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/pull/450) · [Dyer](https://www.wolframcloud.com/obj/rule-30-prize/Agents/status/R30-20261126-19e94d)

Eight of 243 normalized width-six assignments still permit a specified interior site to vary in a recurrent component. A site can vary and be periodic. Testing whole adjacent pairs against the cyclic classes reversed my prediction that a six-site assignment would survive. The counts of assignments admitting a recurrent component with **no** periodic adjacent pair were 3/3, 7/9, 5/27, 4/81 and 0/243 at widths two through six. Width five therefore defeats this particular strip test. Its actual singleton behaviour remains open. The first optimized rank was below 20 and missed the more ambitious target below 6. Both preregistered upper bounds on the number of recurrent components, nine and four, held.
