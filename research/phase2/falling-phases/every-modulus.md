# Two falling residues at every modulus

Fix an integer column `i` and a modulus m ≥ 3. Among the six consecutive columns starting at `i` in the single-seed Rule 30 evolution, one fixed column has falls at two distinct residues modulo `m`, each after every age cutoff. A fall starts at time `t` if the column is black at `t` and white at `t+1`. The chosen strip and modulus come before the choice of column and residues. [Core Lean statement](EveryModulus.lean)

## The reduction to short calendars

Assign each column one permitted residue for its falls. Read the six cells as an integer at every tick and give the strip two freely chosen exterior bits at each update. Call a tick quiet if no column is permitted to fall. If three consecutive updates of a six-cell strip are quiet, the first update fixes the entire strip. Lean checks this directly from the four possible exterior choices at each step. [Strip and quiet-step check](ModulusStrip.lean)

The local reason is that an adjacent black pair forces a fall at its eastern cell. A new interior black cell also produces an adjacent black pair if its black neighbour survives. Two quiet updates therefore require an initial row with no adjacent black cells and no interior birth. Its interior whites have equal neighbours, leaving only an all-white strip or one of the two alternating strips. With three quiet updates the first two rows belong to this list. A quiet step can only add black cells, and an all-white strip can gain black cells only at its ends. So the first two rows agree.

Shift the calendar so the westernmost column's permitted residue is zero. For `m>18`, divide slots 1 through 18 into six disjoint triples. At most five triples can contain a permitted residue. In a vacant triple, delete the first update at the same position in each period. Its row agrees with the next row, so the retained path still satisfies Rule 30 within the strip, with the shortened calendar and two free exterior cells. Reinsert the deleted row in each period to lift an eventually repeating pair from lag `m-1` to lag `m`. Repeating the deletion reduces to 3 ≤ m ≤ 18. [Deletion and reinsertion](ModulusCompress.lean)

One permitted phase modulo `m` implies one permitted phase modulo any divisor of `m`, and a pair that repeats at a divisor also repeats at lag `m`. So the finite check needs only moduli 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13 and 17. It starts with all normalized two-site assignments, with the westernmost phase at zero. At each larger width it checks just the assignments whose two immediate substrip restrictions are both still possible. Every checked certificate assigns a rank to each state and associates a pair of sites and a predicted reading at each phase with that rank. The kernel checks that all allowed edges have non-increasing rank and that all rows match their prediction. On a tail with constant rank, the chosen pair repeats every period. No assignments remain at width six. [Projection](ModulusSlice.lean) · [Rank check](ModulusRank.lean) · [Coverage](ModulusLayers.lean)

If every site of a fixed singleton strip had at most one cofinal falling residue, a common late cutoff would confine all falls to a fixed assignment. The strip argument would make two adjacent singleton columns eventually periodic, which Jen's theorem forbids. [Singleton implication](ModulusSingleton.lean)

## Exact checks and controls

The seven tables certify 918 assignments across widths three through six. An independent checker using the bitwise Rule 30 formula accepted all 134,800 serialized states and 254,586 permitted exterior choices. Merging components whose predictions agree reduced the tables from 1,297,456 to 362,523 bytes. The largest rank is 12 and fits in the six-bit format. The kernel separately verifies which assignments the tables cover. [Generator](build_modulus_tables.py) · [Preregistered scope check](moduli_probe.py) · [Ledger I308–I310 and K96](../../../archive/ledgers/CHAT-LEDGER.13.md)

All seven data modules passed `decide +kernel` and standalone `leanchecker`, and they regenerated byte for byte. The largest separate build, modulus 17, took 141 seconds with other modules building in parallel. Across the seven builds the individual wall times summed to 291 seconds. `EveryModulus` and the parity control also passed standalone replay. The headline uses only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The portal's single-module build and replay still need a timed check at its pin.

A four-row loop, encoded west bit first as 3, 36, 63, 1, is admissible in the width-six graph with falls confined to even ticks. Every adjacent pair differs somewhere under lag two. Thus the free-exterior adjacent-pair assertion fails at modulus two. The status of modulus two on the actual singleton remains open. [Lean parity control](ModulusParity.lean)

The theorem is a submission candidate about the single-seed bulk. The original centre milestone remains open. A nearby witness is compatible with a repeating `011` centre on a different actual row, so a centre proof still needs to use the singleton's moving edge or another feature of its initial row. A centre confined to one falling phase modulo three would eventually parse, after a phase shift, into nondecreasing triples `000`, `001`, `011`, `111`. Ruling out that language would give two cofinal phases at the centre. The original dyadic question also needs two phases within the same shell at arbitrarily late ages. [Realized control](RealizedFlank.lean)

## Credit and replay

KDRonald3 and Claude's *Windows That Always Return* introduced the trapped-window argument with freely varying exterior bits. K96 introduced the modulus parameter, computed the profiles and proposed deletion down to 72. The proof here uses a fixed deletion, so the position of the removed tick agrees across every admissible path, and gives the bound 18. Jen supplies the contradiction on the singleton.
