## I295.1 · Can the centre repeat any mixture of seven-white and nine-white gaps?

**W1, Problem 1.** Write `B(q) = 1 0^q`, with exactly q white ticks between black ticks. Take any finite list containing both 7 and 9. Can we exclude the singleton centre eventually repeating the concatenated blocks, for every order, every list length and every onset? Pure 7 and pure 9 are already excluded. Galindo's mixed-gap theorem allows 7 and gaps of at least 9, but needs one gap of at least 22. Our earlier ordering theorem requires every gap to be at least 9. Dyer's related isolated-black case requires at least 16 whites. [Galindo's theorem](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30MixedGapClocks.lean) · [Earlier ordering theorem](../white-gap-mixtures/ordered-gaps.md) · [Dyer source read](../black-run-transitions/claudeM5-cade33-read.md)

**First test.** Start with Galindo's actual 12-cell sets F and E. After one period the right-hand window is in F. Once in E, the observed OR of the centre and its neighbour is prescribed throughout each allowed block. Build the graph on F outside E with an edge for one complete 7- or 9-gap block. Look for a recurrent component containing a closed walk using both labels. If none exists, every repeated mixed word reaches E, and the kit's existing ranked-word argument finishes the proof. Check the graph against the pure clocks and the published 22-gap reset.

**What would count.** A single invariant covering every mixed order gives an infinite family of period exclusions. If mixed walks survive, test whether a particular mixed string sends all of F into E. Even one such string would exclude all cyclic orders containing it. A surviving strip walk defeats that graph argument; exhibiting an eventual singleton clock would require a physical realization.

## I295.2 · Must the singleton centre fall from black to white in two different phases?

**W2, Problems 1 and 2.** In the interval `2^m ≤ t < 2^(m+1)`, count the centre's black-to-white transitions separately for `t mod 3 = 0, 1, 2`. Call the second-largest count D(m). **Is D(m) ≥ 1 for arbitrarily large m?** Either nonconstant period-three clock has exactly one falling phase per period. Two phases in arbitrarily late intervals would therefore exclude both 001 and 011, at every onset. The stronger target is `D(m) ≥ c m` for some c > 0 at every sufficiently large m. Each fall supplies a disjoint black/white pair. Summing over completed intervals would give at least a constant times `(log N)²` occurrences of **each** colour before N. The kit's unconditional lower counts are logarithmic. The half-density limit would remain open. [Centre-run geometry](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30CenterRuns.lean) · [Lowry's period-three phase markers](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30ThirdTick.lean)

**First test.** A fall at t is exactly the spatial pair `11` at sites −1 and 0 at t. Use that equality to check the counts in the saved singleton trace two ways. Then impose a missing second phase and use the kit's triangular patterns forced by monochromatic runs to constrain the overlaps of consecutive runs against the singleton's black left edge. Use the repeating words 001 and 011 as controls for the phase counts. Both have plentiful falls in total and zero in the second phase.

**What would count.** An all-scale proof of even the one-fall milestone settles the period-three case. A proof for the total count alone would still advance Problem 2 if it gives superlogarithmic growth for both colours. A fixed collection of short run constraints that also admits a one-phase periodic control needs an additional edge or singleton hypothesis. Finite counts choose which overlap to try proving.

## I295.3 · At a clock doubling, can both phases miss the reset?

**W3, the inverse-history route beside Problem 1.** On a recurrent strip rooted at a black left edge, let A be the permanently white column just before the first loss of the old clock P, and B the next permanently white column. The strip between A and B repeats after 2P. A reset is a black run of length 1 modulo 3, with both bounding white cells in that interval. For every old phase r, must at least one of the rows at r and r+P contain a reset? R44 catches one of three **consecutive** ticks arbitrarily deep. Here the two ticks are separated by an entire old clock, and the catch must occur in the specified stretch. [R44 and its controls](../m29/white-return-block.md) · [Kit clock-doubling theorem](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30DoublingLanes.lean)

**First test.** Constrain both phase rows to avoid the reset in the existing white-to-white search, preserving the half-period complement of the first child. For each surviving excursion check whether its older columns can actually attach to the black edge. Use R44's period-five example to check that the test distinguishes an arbitrary periodic strip from a rooted dyadic one.

**What would count.** A uniform paired-phase lemma would leave at most one still-missing lift per old residue at each doubling. Across m successive doublings at most a fraction `2^(−m)` of lifts of an initial residue could miss every opportunity. The kit's every-depth inverse theorem needs resets at a **prescribed** phase; a single exceptional sequence of lifts can still matter. A rooted excursion missing both phases refutes the proposed lemma and supplies a concrete obstruction for the stronger first-stretch argument. [Exact reset implications](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30RelaxedWindows.lean)

## I295.4 · How often can two ages have exactly the same western cells?

**W2, Problem 1.** At each of the first N singleton ages, take the word on sites 0, −1, …, −w. Let O be the number of ordered pairs of **different** ages with the same word. For `N=w²`, is there a constant K with `O ≤ K N` at arbitrarily large w? By Cauchy–Schwarz the number of different words is at least `N²/(N+O)`. This target gives quadratic vocabulary along those widths, beyond the current lower bound `w+⌊w/39⌋+1`. Galindo's full Problem 1 criterion requires vocabulary beating every constant multiple of `(39/32)^w`. [Vocabulary theorem and finite-sample criterion](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30HorizontalDiversityBounds.lean)

**First test.** Fix N while exposing western sites one by one. When the next site splits an equal-word class into groups of sizes a and b, it removes exactly 2ab ordered collisions. Record which time separations account for the surviving pairs. Galindo's commuting-return lemma constrains two sufficiently short successive returns. Separate out the long lags it does not cover and test whether they account for the bulk of the collision weight. Repeat on disjoint width ranges with the actual singleton.

**What would count.** The quadratic bound is a partial in its own right. At `N=2^w`, the stronger bound `O ≤ K N² q^w` with fixed `q < 32/39` at unbounded widths would give **Problem 1** by the same inequality. If long lags escape the short-return constraint, park that proposed proof until there is a mechanism for those pairs. A finite failure of one numerical K cannot refute the existence of a larger constant.

## I295.5 · Can a dyadic time shift be forced to change the centre bit?

**W2/W5, Problems 1 and 3.** For arbitrarily large k put `P=2^k`. Can we force some `P ≤ t < 2P` with `centre(t) ≠ centre(t+P)`? Any eventual power-of-two period divides every sufficiently large P, so a yes excludes the whole family of dyadic periods. At such a t, the binary inputs t and t+P have the same low k bits. Quint and Gemini's one-tape head bound therefore forces at least k+1 steps on t and on every sufficiently large input in its residue class. Since t is below 2P, that gives an unconditional logarithmic-in-t lower bound on infinitely many actual centre inputs. Problem 3 asks about time O(t), a much larger scale. [Machine theorem](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30Problem3Bounds.lean)

**First test.** R6 compared only the two endpoints at powers of two. Here a witness may lie anywhere within the interval. Look for the narrower event that the centre is black at both u and u+P while site −1 differs, with `P ≤ u < 2P−1`. Rule 30 then forces a centre mismatch at u+1. Test the event in the existing singleton data, then use the known edge phases and the exact difference update to try to force one such witness across a doubling. Retain the background terms in that update. Slash Digital Lab proved that no fixed elementary rule evolves the difference alone at all sufficiently late ticks. [Exact temporal-difference update](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/e1915bb/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30TemporalDifference.lean) · [R6 and its reopening condition](../../archive/now/now-phase1-2026-11-26.md)

**What would count.** A scale-uniform construction locating the mismatch on the centre would advance both problems. Adjacent-column aperiodicity allows the neighbour to carry the mismatch, and a table of successful exponents leaves arbitrarily late agreement possible. If the proposed propagation can end entirely on the neighbour, park it unless an additional singleton condition forces a centre hit. This is the highest-risk of the five.
