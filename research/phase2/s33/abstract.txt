On a recurrent Rule 30 left-edge strip, let wheel a be permanently white, let the next wheel first lose the clock of period 2ᵉ, and let b be the next permanently white wheel. In every five consecutive ticks, three successive wheels between a and min(b, a + 7) read white, black, white (`Rule30Submission.ClockPhasePairs.five_ticks`). Of any three consecutive ticks and their translates by 2ᵉ, one has such a singleton by min(b, a + 6) (`Rule30Submission.ClockPhasePairs.three_pairs`). On a strip pointed by a black edge, one fixed tick of any five sees these singletons at arbitrarily deep first losses (`Rule30Submission.ClockPhasePairs.cofinal_singletons`). All three statements hold for every clock exponent and every starting tick. Quint and Gemini proved the post-loss cascade and a catch after each driver fall (`Rule30RelaxedWindows`), catches at every tick for clocks up to 16 (`Rule30DoublingLanes`), and catches after long constant runs (`Rule30FirstLossBounds`). Together these give a reset in fifteen ticks within nine wheels. The present proof follows the driver through three cases, a fall, two black readings or three white readings, each of which forces a toggling wheel whose next fall is isolated. It uses no computation. It leaves open whether the prescribed tick in `DLMinusOne` must succeed, and Problem 1 is not claimed.
