A column of Rule 30 from a single black cell falls at tick t when it is black at t and white at t + 1. For every modulus m ≥ 2 and any 27 consecutive columns, one fixed column has falls at ticks of two different residues modulo m, each after every cutoff (`Rule30Submission.PhaseFalls.Moduli.two_cofinal_every_modulus_from_two`). For m ≥ 3 six consecutive columns suffice (`two_cofinal_every_modulus`, the posted entry this one supersedes). The new case is m = 2: among any 27 consecutive columns, one falls both on even and on odd ticks, arbitrarily late (`two_parities_every_27`). If every column of a strip kept its late falls to one residue, the strip would follow a finite graph with both exterior cells free. Jen's theorem forbids two eventually periodic singleton columns with any periods, so it is enough that every run of that graph has an adjacent pair that repeats with some lag. At m = 2 the six-cell strip admits a four-row loop with every fall on an even tick (`not_good_parity`). It has period four, and is the reason that wider strips and lags other than two are needed. Fifteen kernel-checked certificates, of widths 4 to 20, give each node a rank, a class and a position. The six widest are checked only on the product of the states that the two halves of the strip eventually occupy. A classification by substrips then covers every assignment of 27 cells. A faster sound checker carries the same certificates for m ≥ 3. The column is not fixed, and the centre-only and period-two questions stay open.
