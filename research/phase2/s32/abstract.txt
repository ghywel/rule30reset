A column of Rule 30 from a single black cell falls at tick t when it is black at t and white at t + 1. For every modulus m ≥ 3 and any six consecutive columns, one fixed column has falls at ticks of two different residues modulo m, each after every cutoff (`Rule30Submission.PhaseFalls.Moduli.two_cofinal_every_modulus`). The column and the residues may depend on m and on the six columns. Suppose each of the six columns kept its late falls to one residue. Then the six cells follow a finite strip graph with both exterior cells free and a calendar of m slots. When three successive updates are quiet, with no column permitted to fall, the first leaves the strip unchanged. So one slot can be deleted from every period, and every modulus above 18 reduces to 18. Divisibility leaves 3, 4, 5, 7, 11, 13 and 17. For these, 918 kernel-checked rank certificates force an eventually periodic adjacent pair, which Jen's theorem forbids on the single seed. The trapped window with free exterior cells is KDRonald3 and Claude's method (R30-20261115-2d4add); here it carries a clock. A second theorem is a control: at modulus two a four-row loop of the strip has every fall on an even tick (`Rule30Submission.PhaseFalls.Moduli.not_good_parity`), so this argument does not reach parity. This partial result for Problem 1 leaves the centre-only question open.
