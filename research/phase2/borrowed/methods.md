# K92.5 · What transfers at a white tail or on a single orbit?

**The inverse-counting comparison gives a test we can actually state for Rule 30.** At arbitrarily large exact widths of each parity, can some finite observation leave at most 4,095 rows passing every black-phase check? A yes implies Q01 and excludes an eventually alternating seed centre. The observation horizon may depend on the width. This is a corollary of the published immortal-row amplification theorem. Finding the two unbounded sequences of widths is open. ### The inverse-counting test, with quantifiers

Write E(w,k) for the rows of **exact bitlength** w whose nearest western cell is black at times 0,2,…,2(k−1) under the white boundary. Suppose that for each parity and every lower width bound B there exist w≥B of that parity and a finite k with fewer than 4,096 distinct rows in E(w,k). If Q01 fails, take a finite immortal row of width b. The published theorem supplies 4,096 distinct immortals at every w≥b+389 of its parity (first possible width b+390). All pass every finite k. Applying the assumed bound at one such w is a contradiction. The new check in this block is the Lean formulation of this short deduction. The inverse multiplicity and its explicit threshold are the authors' result.

The 3,517 survivors at width **at most** 48 in K92.2 do upper-bound the corresponding exact-width class for that one width and horizon. A finite table cannot provide cofinal widths of either parity. For the second block, try to build a recurrence for an upper bound on E(w,k(w)) on two subsequences of widths, one per parity. Test each proposed recurrence against the hybrid and planted rules and specify the exact Rule 30 identity that justifies it. An inverse lower bound with no upper estimate leaves the argument open.
