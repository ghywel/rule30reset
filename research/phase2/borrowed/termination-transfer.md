# K92.5 · Termination, undecidability and erosion

## 3. Can a termination prover use the wall word?

Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule encode Collatz in a mixed binary–ternary string rewriting system. Their Theorem 3.17 is an equivalence. Theorems 3.8 and 3.10 exclude natural matrix interpretations for an earlier unary encoding under the paper's two proof disciplines. Theorems 4.9 and 4.10 show what a successful weakening looks like: a hypothetical nonconvergent trajectory must visit each of certain residue classes. Relative termination permits strict descent on selected rules and weak descent on the others. The representation of the states and the choice of strict steps both matter. Read the authors' full text, §§2.2–2.3, 3.1–3.2 and 4.3–4.4. [Yolcu, Aaronson and Heule](https://emreyolcu.com/research/rewriting-collatz.pdf)

For Rule 30 write `F(y) = (y >> 1) xor (y or (y << 1))` for the white-wall update, with depth one in bit zero, and `G = F ∘ F`. A surviving step has both `y` and `G(y)` odd. On an infinite surviving run, the odd-tick depth-one bit equals the complement of bit one of the preceding even-tick row. The kit proves that the odd-tick stream of any such finite start would be non-eventually-periodic. It would therefore visit both colours cofinally, and its packed even-tick rows would visit **both 1 and 3 modulo 4 cofinally**. Thus a natural rank weakly decreasing on all surviving steps and strictly decreasing on either chosen residue class would suffice for Q01. This implication is checked in core Lean using the kit's theorem. **PROVED IN LEAN.** The exact trajectory `3 → 6 → 13 → 25 → 55` gives the following obstruction. Read the binary word of each even-tick row from its outer edge toward the wall.

| Source mod 4 | Surviving double step | Edge-first binary words |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 3 → 13 | 11 → 1101 |
| 1 | 13 → 55 | 1101 → 110111 |

Each old word is a prefix of the next one. Let each letter act by any order-preserving map on a partially ordered set with least element ⊥. Evaluate a word by composition from left to right, so `[uv](⊥) = [u]([v](⊥))`. This is at least `[u](⊥)`. An order-preserving readout into the naturals cannot fall on either displayed step. In particular this rules out arbitrarily large nonnegative affine letter matrices evaluated on the zero vector in any finite dimension, including products with infinite image. It also rules out the variant that requires descent only on one of the two cofinal classes. The witnesses concern this edge-first encoding and its terminal value.

In dimension one the conclusion for nonnegative affine maps extends to **any** fixed natural terminal value. Write `T_i(x) = a_i x + b_i`. If both slopes are positive, both maps are inflationary and the two prefix witnesses work for any terminal. If `a_1 = 0`, every positive row begins with 1 and receives the same value. If `a_0 = 0`, every row beginning with 110 receives the same value. For class 1 use 13 → 55 as above. For class 3 use `27 → 50 → 111` under F: the even rows are `11011` and `1101111`. This exhausts the cases. [Checked exclusions for bottom-initialized ranks and scalar affine ranks](PrefixRank.lean)

The kit separately excludes width plus any *finite*-monoid image, with optional fixed edge windows, for ranks that drop at every surviving double step. Its free-middle theorem excludes lower-bounded additive local-window ranks before the last growing margin of the row has been read. Its gap barrier excludes ranks through finite abstractions even during repeated five-gaps with fixed lookahead. Each theorem specifies its population and its strict steps. A concrete remaining test is an edge-first interpretation with **two** nonnegative affine coordinates and a nonzero vector supplied by an end marker. Require coordinatewise weak descent on every surviving G and strict descent of the first coordinate only at 3 modulo 4. Screen first on the three exact steps above. A fit becomes a proof only after checking its inequalities at arbitrary widths. This is a proposed test. ## 4. What exactly is undecidable?

Delacourt and Ollinger vary the automaton and its alphabet. Even for a reversible one-way automaton with a quiescent background and a single exceptional cell, determining whether a designated state ever occurs anywhere is undecidable (Theorem 1). Determining whether its **whole orbit** is periodic is also undecidable (Theorem 2). Their construction simulates multi-head walking automata with a layered alphabet. Cervelle, Formenti and Guillon vary a binary cellular automaton and allow the radius to vary. Their Theorem 5.6 concerns a property of the **set of all column traces**: if nontrivial on binary CA traces and invariant under ultimate coincidence of the trace sets, its decision problem is undecidable. Read the former's §§2, 4–6 and the latter's §5 in the primary texts. Both results appear in our earlier archive. [Delacourt and Ollinger](https://www.univ-orleans.fr/lifo/Members/delacourt/files/cie2017.pdf) · [Cervelle, Formenti and Guillon](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.0251)

Neither supplies an independence result for Rule 30, nor an undecidability theorem for its single-seed centre. The elementary radius-one binary rule class itself has only 256 members. The relevant obstruction to a proposed Q01 argument is specific: exhibit an immortal finite row in a system satisfying every proposed hypothesis. The alternating 150/90 space hybrid does that for edge advance and the two-tick spatial reset. The planted survivor does it for any prescribed finite table of Rule 30 width/horizon counts. These tests identify the missing hypothesis for that argument. [Control laws and their precise scopes](../../m10/control-laws.md)

## 7. Can time blocking turn Rule 30 into a monotone eroder?

For a monotone binary CA, Toom characterizes erosion of all finite islands of ones in zeros by the geometry of zero-forcing sets. In a synchronous one-step rule the intersection of their spatial convex hulls must be empty. The criterion in the author's more general spacetime notation is that the intersection of the corresponding convex rays consists only of the origin. Read the definitions and Theorem 3 in §6.3 of Toom's own exposition. Monotonicity is essential to that characterization. Nonmonotone eroders exist: elementary rule 8 outputs one exactly on 011. Its outputs can never be adjacent, so a second tick makes the entire line white. [Toom, *Cellular Automata with Errors*](https://cs-web.bu.edu/faculty/gacs/toomandre-com-backup/my-articles/engmat/SNELL.PDF)

**PROVED IN LEAN, all positive time blocks.** At time zero place bits `a,b` at sites `−t,+t` and leave all other sites white. At time `t > 0` the centre reads **`a xor b`**. With `a=0`, all-white gives zero and a singleton at `+t` gives one because its leftmost black moves one cell west per tick. Flipping `a` always flips the output by the kit's leftmost-ancestor theorem. These four finite starts prove the assertion for every t. A two-input XOR stays XOR or XNOR under independent input and output complements. Neither is coordinatewise monotone. The restriction therefore prevents every positive iterate of the raw Rule 30 rule from being monotone, including in a translated frame or with sitewise choices of colour polarity. [Two-endpoint obstruction](BlockedErosion.lean) · [Permutive difference front](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30CoupledEmptyRight.lean) · [Moving singleton edge](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30FiniteConfigs.lean)

Even comparing two rows on a prescribed background leaves their rightmost differing cell moving one step east forever. A full-line monotone eroder that majorized the whole evolving disagreement from a finite island would therefore contradict this exact front law. A possible erosion argument must follow a **restricted** defect relevant to the observed wall. It must specify when that defect reaches the observation site. Q01 asks for one white even-tick reading there, while a full-space island may persist indefinitely.
