# K98 families 6, 8 and 9 · What randomness and computation transfer to the seed?

## 6. Which ergodic results include Rule 30?

**Known.** Rule 30 is a nonlinear example of a deterministic cellular automaton mixing to every order under the invariant fair product measure. Its local rule permutes the leftmost input at position −1. Chang's Remark 3.2 extends the corner argument to nonlinear rules. The archive had already credited Shereshevsky's one-dimensional Theorem 1.3.3 for precisely this result. The kit proves the stronger finite statement that sufficiently separated windows are exactly independent. In particular, any initial law with a density relative to the fair product measure converges weakly to it under iteration: apply mixing first to simple densities and then approximate in mean. The singleton has probability zero under the fair measure. [Chang, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1410.2144#page=12) · [kit, Mixing](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/equivalents/Rule30Equivalents/Mixing.lean) · [prior read of Shereshevsky](../../../PRIOR-ART-ARCHIVE.md#the-owners-wide-survey-nature-biology-and-the-other-disciplines-surveyed-2026-10-05-by-cloud)

Two other theorems answer different questions about the law of the starting row.

- **Deterministic randomization and rigidity.** Host, Maass and Martínez prove convergence of Cesàro means for particular right-permutative rules with extra algebraic structure. In their affine–translation product, the affine factor becomes uniform and a shift-invariant marginal remains on the translation factor. The starting law is translation invariant with complete connections and summable decay. Sablik's rigidity theorem characterizes certain jointly invariant positive-entropy measures for algebraic *bipermutative* rules. For Rule 30 the right neighbour is erased when the centre is black, and its singleton point mass fails translation invariance. Neither theorem supplies a deterministic singleton limit. [Host, Maass and Martínez, Theorem 5.12 and Corollaries 5.13–14](https://www.aimsciences.org/article/doi/10.3934/dcds.2003.9.1423) · [Sablik, Theorems 3.3–3.4](https://www.math.univ-toulouse.fr/~msablik/article/2007-etds.pdf)
- **Positive noise at every tick.** Marcovici, Sablik and Taati allow either left or right permutivity, including nonlinear rules. After each update, independently flipping each output with fixed probability strictly between zero and one gives uniform ergodicity, with the fair product law as unique invariant measure. The result includes the singleton as the starting row of the *noisy* evolution. Its proof couples a finite window through a strictly positive transition kernel. [Marcovici, Sablik and Taati, §3.6, Theorem 3.16 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.05500#page=24)

### How much may the initial row agree with the singleton?

**PROOF-SKETCH, a deduction from the kit's leftmost-ancestor argument.** Fix the entire initial row from site −R eastwards to the singleton. Draw independent fair bits only west of −R. At the centre, ages 0 through R match the actual seed. Each subsequent age t introduces the fresh bit at −t, which is XORed with a function of sites strictly to its east. This follows by following the left edge of the backward light cone. Consequently the entire later trace consists of independent fair bits. With B(T) the true number of black centre cells at ages 0 through T−1, and Q = min(T,R+1), the exact expected black fraction at every T ≥ 1 is

$$\frac12+\frac{B(Q)-Q/2}{T}.$$

For R = o(T) this tends to one half for *every* rule with this leftmost XOR, including the shift (Rule 240) whose singleton centre stays white after age zero. For R ≥ T−1 the expression equals B(T)/T. If Q/T tends to a positive constant, the discrepancy at Q is multiplied by that constant in the limit. These are three different experiments at the same observation site. [Kit, leftmost ancestor and distinct-foot proof](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/equivalents/Rule30Equivalents/Mixing.lean)

Even a set of random sites of asymptotic density zero can suffice. Fix every initial site except −s for s in any infinite set S of positive integers. Write independent fair bits there. At each fixed site i, the samples at nonnegative ages t = i+s are independent fair bits: reveal the western bits in increasing order of s; each new bit complements just the new observation among those exposed. Almost surely every column differs from each specified computable sequence, by countability of the sites and the algorithms. This holds even for S = {2^(2^j) : j ≥ 1}. With independent flip probabilities p_s, the probability of matching any prescribed column through the first k relevant samples is at most the product of 1−min(p_s,1−p_s) over those samples. Divergence of the sum of these minima suffices for the same exclusion. With summable flip probabilities p_s < 1 the untouched singleton instead has strictly positive probability, namely the product of 1−p_s. Any almost-sure aperiodicity result for that last ensemble would already contain the prize seed. Rule 240 exhibits the obstruction on its untouched atom. [Kit, trace plus right half is bijective](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/equivalents/Rule30Equivalents/RandomConfigurations.lean)

## 8. Can one bit sequence have a provable lower bound on a strong machine?

**Yes, even for individually requested bits on binary input.** The kit's formal Problem 3 forbids every bound C(n+1) for the single bit c(n) on its one-tape machine with the binary index least significant digit first. A multitape machine printing an entire prefix has a different input and output specification. Hennie and Stearns' Theorem 3 constructs a single sequence with a separation for prefix generation by diagonalization. The following example addresses the kit's input convention directly. [Kit, Problem 3](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30Problem3.lean) · [Hennie and Stearns, Theorems 2–3](https://www.khoury.northeastern.edu/~viola/classes/papers/HennieStearns66.pdf)

**Known theorem and coding corollary.** Fischer and Rabin's Theorem 1 gives a fixed c > 0 such that every decider for Presburger truth, at every sufficiently large input length L, takes more than 2^(2^(cL)) steps on some sentence. Their formulas already have binary encodings. Define one total sequence by stripping the leading 1 from the usual binary expansion of n and returning 1 exactly when the remaining word encodes a true sentence; assign 0 to invalid codes and n=0. A length-L formula gives an index below 2^(L+1), convertible to least-significant-first order in polynomial time. Any fixed multitape lookup algorithm with time polynomial in the numeric index would decide Presburger truth in 2^O(L) time, even after the polynomial overhead of simulation by the paper's one-tape machine. This contradicts Theorem 1. Here the bits of the index select an arbitrary hard sentence. [Fischer and Rabin, Theorem 1, machine model in §2 and coding in Theorem 6](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/0/03/Super-exponential_Complexity_of_Presburger_Arithmetic_by_Fischer_and_Rabin_%281974%29_-_MIT-LCS-TM-043.pdf)

For a sequence defined without encoding decisions, Adamczewski, Cassaigne and Le Gonidec exclude every deterministic pushdown transducer for the digits of an algebraic irrational, such as the fractional part of √2. Their transducer receives the index most-significant digit first. Their theorem restricts the machine model and supplies no unrestricted time lower bound for those digits. [Theorem 1.3, input convention §2 and Definition 2.12](https://adamczewski.perso.math.cnrs.fr/HS.pdf)

**Transfer obstruction.** A polynomial-time reduction sending arbitrary length-L Presburger sentences to binary time indices for Rule 30's singleton is impossible. Its output would have polynomially many bits, hence value at most 2^poly(L). Direct simulation of the triangular light cone takes polynomial time in that value (O(n²) cell updates). Composing the two would decide Presburger truth in 2^poly(L), again below Fischer–Rabin. A reduction from a weaker hard language would need an explicit encoding, a proof of its correctness on the unaltered seed, and a size bound sharp enough to imply the proposed time lower bound. Conversely, the binary Champernowne construction already in our archive shows that even full normality permits digit lookup in time polynomial in the index's bit length. [Archived Champernowne comparison](../../../PRIOR-ART-ARCHIVE.md#2026-10-08--gpt-gc542-normality-versus-indexed-computation) · [Fischer and Rabin, Theorem 1](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/2f7b4975-459f-4dc7-9f70-2107d97bd8d1)

## 9. A weaker statement in the prize's machine model

**PROVED IN LEAN, a corollary of two kit theorems.** For a physical starting row with a leftmost black cell, at all but at most one fixed site i the following holds. For every k ≥ 1 and every correct kit machine for that site's trace, there is an input r in [2^(k−1), 3·2^(k−1)) requiring at least k+1 steps. At that same input the machine reaches tape cell k, giving right span at least k+1. Jen gives at most one eventually periodic column, hence every other column fails each dyadic period. Apply Quint and Gemini's canonical-window right-span bound. Once the machine halts its span stays fixed, and a run with span k+1 has taken at least k+1 steps. Thus the same index witnesses both bounds, for every correct time allowance. The same site works for every k. For each fixed site of a finite start, a digit machine exists by direct light-cone simulation. This includes the actual singleton and leaves open which site is the possible exception. The bound itself is compatible with period three: the indicator of multiples of three changes under every dyadic shift at arbitrarily late multiples of three. [Core Lean statement and constant-trace control](ColumnCost.lean) · [Kit, Jen for left-finite configurations](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30FiniteConfigs.lean) · [Kit, canonical-window time and right-span bounds](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30Problem3Bounds.lean)

**A specified lookup on the centre bond.** Define a single Boolean sequence by taking the actual centre colour at index 2t and the OR of sites 0 and 1 at index 2t+1 (both at physical age t). It has the same all-scale lower bound at a **named** bond. An eventual period P of the interleaving would give a shift by 2P from every late index, so both readings would have eventual period P in physical time. This violates the kit's masked Jen theorem. The proof works at every fixed bond on every row with a leftmost black. It combines the kit's interleaving operation, masked Jen, and Quint and Gemini's machine theorem. Computing just the even-indexed part remains the original centre problem. [Core Lean](ColumnCost.lean) · [Kit, masked Jen](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30FiniteMaskedJen.lean) · [The ternary bond's two readings](TernaryRoot.lean)

This would force logarithmic lookup time on infinitely many binary indices for the actual centre. The missing step is an all-scale reason for that discrepancy.
