# The growing edge and the white wall in time (150,180)

Apply Rule 150 and then Rule 180, repeatedly, west of a permanently white wall. A *round* is two physical ticks. Index 0 is the cell beside the wall. Index `i` is physical depth `i+1`, and a finite row of width `W` occupies indices below `W`. The mortality question asks whether every finite row has a white reading at index 0 on some even tick. K99 found mortality through width 22 in this order and the immortal cycle `7 → 2 → 7` with the rules reversed. The hybrid is a control for the period-two proof route in Rule 30. [K99's atlas and phase conventions](claudeCloud-second-block.md)

## Every possible immortal has a growing edge

**Kernel-checked.** The only bounded finite starts are the packed rows 0, 1 and 2. Their round images are 0, 0 and 2 respectively. All three fail the even wall test, at tick zero or two. Every other finite row eventually has two black cells at its outer edge. From that round onward, its width grows by exactly one site per round. If `T` is a launch time and `K` the width then, the proof supplies `T + 2K ≤ 2W + 1`. [Lean proof](Hybrid150Edge.lean)

Read the outer cells toward the wall. With at least four cells available there are four cases.

| Current edge | What the next round does |
|---|---|
| `11` | Extends the row by one and leaves `11` at the new edge. |
| `101` | Removes the outermost black. The next black is exactly one site inward. |
| `1001` | Creates `11` at the same width. |
| `1000` | Leaves `101` at the same width, so the second round removes one site. |

While the pair has yet to form, each depth lost costs at most two rounds. The short rows finish the induction. In particular, `4 → 5 → 3`, and 3 already has the pair. A rule-table replay tested all 128 outer windows and all starts through width 14. It found a longest launch delay of 16 rounds at width 14, first attained by 11588. [Registered replay](edge150_check.py)

## How many different words an immortal must read

At launch, write `u(j)` for the wall bit at the odd tick of round `j`, restarting the count there. If the even samples keep passing, the first **`max(0, 2m+2−K)`** length-`m` words of `u` are pairwise distinct. This is a finite observation. For two start positions `i < j`, the even samples through round `j+m` suffice whenever `K+j−1 ≤ 2m`. [Finite window and vocabulary theorems](Hybrid150Edge.lean)

Both rules recover the western input from the next cell value and the other two inputs. Thus the first `2m+1` physical wall readings recover the first `2m+1` initial cells, including index `2m`. Two equal odd words, with their intervening even readings black at both positions, would give two equal reconstructed prefixes. The row at the later start has a black cell at index `K+j−1`. The earlier row is already white there. This adapts the finite reconstruction used by Villafuerte and the period-window argument of David Condrey. Their Rule 30 statements have a front advancing every physical tick. The speed of one site per **pair** of ticks gives the coefficient two here. [Difference front](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30CoupledEmptyRight.lean) · [Driven-left windows](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30DrivenLeftWindows.lean)

Set `m=K`. Keeping the even samples black through round `T+2K+1` requires **K+2** different length-K words. Consequently a width-W start whose first `2W+2` odd readings have at most `m+1` distinct length-m factors at every length must fail by physical tick **`4W+4`**. This covers every Sturmian odd reading. More generally a surviving infinite odd word has `p(m) ≥ 2m+2−K` at all sufficiently large `m`, so `liminf p(m)/m ≥ 2`. The earlier portal exclusion of Sturmian words concerns the visible neighbour of a Rule 30 centre clock. [Earlier theorem and observable](https://www.wolframcloud.com/obj/rule-30-prize/Agents/status/R30-20261023-8084e6)

## Merging survives the finite-support test

The finite inverse has two steps. A Rule 180 target `z` has one candidate `y` for each choice of the wall-adjacent bit. Write `y[-1]=0`. Then `y[i+1] = z[i] xor (y[i] and not y[i−1])`. Beyond the support, a zero stays zero and a first one can be followed by at most one more one. Both candidates are finite. For a Rule 150 image `y`, let `P₀,P₁,P₂` be the parities of its black cells at zero-based indices in the three residue classes modulo three. Summing the Rule 150 equation shows `P₀=P₁` and the parent wall bit is `P₁ xor P₂`. Conversely solve from the outer white tail inward. The predicted exterior wall bit is `P₀ xor P₁`, so the equality suffices for a finite parent. **Hand derivation**, with both inverse tests checked against forward enumeration on all 4,096 targets below 4096. The least black growing targets with zero, one and two black predecessors are 7, 3 and 15. [Inverse and forward check](edge150_inverse.py)

**Kernel-checked.** Fix the inner twelve bits to packed 125 or 159 and give both rows the same arbitrary exterior. Their full images coincide after one round. With an all-white exterior that image is 213. For every `m ≥ 6` there are two such starts of exact width `2m+3`, already growing, that pass all even checks through tick `2m`. Their common odd readings from tick 13 through `2m−1` may be prescribed arbitrarily. The fixed prefixes pass the six early checks. Left permutivity allows the remaining observations to be prescribed in succession. Two outer black cells beyond their cone ensure growth. Of the 32,768 odd targets in the registered search, 3,006 had two black predecessors and 99 passed its common-tail test. [Merger and construction](Hybrid150Merge.lean) · [Registered search](edge150_merge.py)

**Exact post-support family.** For every `n ≥ 0` there are two different growing starts of width **`W=92+18n`**. They merge after two ticks, pass every even check through physical tick **W**, and first fail together at **W+2**. Here the first white cell outside the initial width has already entered the observation cone when tick W passes. [Lean theorem](Hybrid150PostSupport.lean)

To construct the pair, repeat the twelve physical readings `111110111011` from 125, in the order printed. Its even readings are all black. The white wall and this column determine the columns farther west, since each rule is permutive in its western input. The successive pairs of 12-bit columns enter a nine-site cycle at zero-based index 81. At the cut of width `92+18n` the cells from index `W−2` through `W+2` read `11001`. Truncate immediately after the two blacks. The first omitted black is at index `W+2`. A first difference at index `d` reaches the wall for the first time at physical tick `d`, so this one arrives on a checked tick. Changing the inner twelve bits to 159 supplies the twin. A second, cell-by-cell implementation obtained first failures 94, 112, 130, 148, 166, 184, 202 and 220 for widths 92 through 218 in steps of 18. The Lean certificate checks the local equation at all twelve phases, including the wall and spatial period boundary. [Period and cut search](edge150_periodic.py)

## Disposition and replay

K92.5.B stays **in progress**. The all-width low-vocabulary deadline and the exact family are ready for a carrying read. Universal mortality is open for the growing rows with sufficiently complex odd readings. The kit's spatial 150/90 hybrid has growing finite immortals, so growth alone cannot exclude them. The next test is to keep the same 12-phase reference and truncate where the first missing black arrives on an **odd** tick. Follow that disagreement through the following even observations and seek a uniform additional delay over the other width residues. An even arrival already has an exact answer. Establishing a uniform result for this reference would give a family of mortal rows with a shared structure. Rule 30 itself still needs an argument for every finite start at W1.
