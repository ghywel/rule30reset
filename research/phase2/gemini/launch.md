# Where marked gaps come from

**THEOREM (Lean).** Every positive-width mark in the singleton Rule 30 spacetime descends from a black run read westward from site $-2$ to its first white neighbour. The update immediately after that run creates the white gap. Later quiet updates can record the same future black arrival again, at successively smaller widths. This gives an exact choice of which rows to count and a lower bound for the number of centre ticks of each colour. [Lean proofs](MarkLaunch.lean)

**Four cells detect a birth of width at least two.** At sites $-3,-2,-1,0$ the words are precisely **1101, 1110, 1111**. Each begins with 11 and has at least one black cell in the centre pair. The black run extending west from site $-2$ must meet a white cell because the seed has finite support. If these three words occur $k$ times before $N$, each centre colour occurs at least $\lceil k/2\rceil$ times before $N+3$. Counting all four words beginning with 11 also gives two-sided bounds, with a smaller coefficient for black. Thus the frequency question can use ordinary temporal falls at a single site two cells west.

## Finding the birth

Write `Gap(s,l)` when row $s$ is black at $-l-1$ and white from $-l$ through $-1$. Lowry's `Mark(s,l)` additionally requires a black centre at $s+l$. Call the pair at $(-1,0)$ active when either cell is black. Write `Bar(t,l)` when this pair is active, $-l-2$ is white, and all $l$ cells from $-l-1$ through $-2$ are black.

For $l>0$, `next_gap_iff` gives precisely two ways to see `Gap(t+1,l)`. A quiet pair shortens an existing gap of width $l+1$ by one. An active pair creates a gap whose width equals the number of consecutive black cells read westward starting at $-2$. The cases specialize the kit's `Rule30SettledRepeats.back` older/newborn white-run dichotomy to the fixed centre. Walking the quiet case backwards preserves the arrival time. It must terminate because the seed row has no black cap west of the centre. Thus a mark at $(s,l)$ has a most recent active tick $t<s$ with a bar of width $w\ge l$ and $t+1+w=s+l$. The gap at $t+1$ itself is marked. [Kit white-run dichotomy](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30SettledRepeats.lean) · [Lowry's marks](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30MarkedGaps.lean)

For the two width-22 marks at ages 34,429 and 2,125,919 in the existing census, the preceding rows therefore both have a white cell at $-24$, black cells at $-23,\ldots,-2$, and an active pair. An inherited mark would require an earlier width-23 mark, above the census maximum. Lowry had already certified the mark at 34,429 and the echo on its birth row 34,428 in `Rule30OffCenterEcho.singleton_mark_and_echo`. The deduction about both birth rows uses the recorded census and `record_is_birth`. ## Which births are marked?

On the bar row, retain the cells from $-1$ to $l+1$, continue black to their west, and compute the centre $l+1$ ticks later. The answer is exact because the entire western part of its light cone is the known bar. Thus unbounded marks are *equivalent* to arbitrarily wide bars on the singleton orbit whose eastern context passes this finite test. Combining this with Lowry's criterion gives `problem1_of_births`. This covers both edge-matching and other contexts. It still requires a full cone evaluation in general. M220's short edge prefix is a cheaper sufficient test for certain contexts. Neither supplies a proof that qualifying bars recur at unbounded widths. [M220's prefix test and limitations](../marks/claudeM5-edge-predictor.md)

## What a count would imply

* A bar at $t$ of width at least $2d$ excludes any other bar of width at least $2d$ through $t+d$. The first update clears an interval whose shrinking white interior contains site $-d-1$ at each intervening tick. A second bar would need that site black.
* If two distinct births share the arrival time and their widths in chronological order are $l,m$, then $l\ge 2m+3$. The older gap's black cap advances one cell per tick while protected by its white interior. At the younger birth it would occupy the required white flank if $l<2m+3$. In particular, birth-to-arrival times are injective among bars in any one band $[L,2L+2]$.
* Every bar of width at least two forces **both** centre colours in $[t,t+3]$, independently of the later arrival. The last black bar cell and the active pair force a black centre by $t+2$. Four consecutive black centre cells starting at $t$ require site $-3$ to be white at $t$, contrary to the bar. For width at least six these four-tick windows are disjoint by the first result with $d=3$. At width at least two, starts are at least two ticks apart. Any centre tick can therefore belong to at most two birth windows. [Kit black-triangle proof](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30CenterRuns.lean)

Let $B_w(N)$ count distinct births of width at least $w$ before $N$. Before $N+3$, the centre records each colour at least $\max(B_6(N),\lceil B_2(N)/2\rceil)$ times. For the width-two bound, assign each birth a witness tick of the desired colour and label whether that tick comes in the first or second pair of its four-tick window. Two births sharing the same tick and label would have to be at most one tick apart. A positive lower density $\delta$ of width-at-least-two births would therefore put the centre's lower and upper black frequencies within $[\delta/2,1-\delta/2]$. Width one cannot give this conclusion from the local birth condition alone. On the fixed whole-line checkerboard with a black centre, a width-one bar occurs at every tick while the centre stays black. This example has a different initial row from the singleton.

## Counting falls without the active-pair condition

Write $F(N)$ for the number of black-to-white transitions starting before $N$ at site $-2$. Rule 30 gives a fall exactly when $-3,-2$ read 11, since the new value of a black cell is the complement of its western neighbour. S32 supplies a result about bands of columns. Here the count must grow at this particular column. [S32 review and its quantifiers](../falling-phases/claudeCloud-review.md)

Every fall supplies a white centre within three ticks by Pérez Morales's black-triangle law. Falls are at least two ticks apart, so $F(N)\le 2\,\#\mathrm{white}_0(N+3)$. Ulrey's `center_two_step_black_independent_of_right` already identifies the centre two ticks later with the current bit at −2 when the centre is black and −1 is white. The cumulative estimate below counts arbitrary ticks. [Ulrey's two-block result](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/10a4b86/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30TwoBlockCounts.lean)

For black, let $A_t,C_t,E_t$ be the 0/1 values at $-2,0,+1$. A five-cell truth table gives

$$ A_t+E_t\ \le\ E_{t+1}+2C_t+C_{t+1}+C_{t+2}. $$

To see the zero case, during three white centre ticks the western and eastern neighbours agree at the first two times. The eastern neighbour can only rise during the white run. The Rule 30 update at $-1$ then makes $A_t$ precisely that rise. The kit's white-run reconstruction gives the corresponding interior pattern. The displayed inequality includes the boundary ticks and sums over arbitrary runs. Summing cancels the eastern-neighbour terms and leaves $F(N)\le \#\mathrm{black}_{-2}(N)\le 4\,\#\mathrm{black}_0(N+2)+1$. The Lean file checks the truth table, the sum and the white counting argument. [Pérez Morales's run geometry](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30CenterRuns.lean)

If the lower density of falls at $-2$ is $\rho>0$, the centre's lower black frequency is at least $\rho/4$ and its upper black frequency at most $1-\rho/2$. Even a lower bound $F(N)/\log N\to\infty$ would improve the logarithmic counts for both colours. The singleton's fall rate at this fixed site remains open. Unbounded marked widths alone give no rate in time, and exact half density requires more.
