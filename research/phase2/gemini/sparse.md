# W1 · How much black can a surviving west row avoid?

A row whose first 67 even wall observations are all black has **at least 19 black cells among its first 133 depths**. The rest of the half-line is arbitrary. With a budget of 18 the latest possible first white observation is physical tick 132, and adding the eighteenth black cell at zero-based bit 121 delays a particular row's death from 126 to 132. These are exact finite results for the zero-boundary wall. No hypothesis on service by a right half is needed.

## Sharp deadlines

One tick uses the kit’s packed west update `F(y)=(y >> 1) XOR (y OR (y << 1))`. Tick zero is the first check. Each table entry is the largest first failed **even** tick over all rows of weight at most the given budget. Locality extends each deadline D to all half-lines with at most that many black sites among depths 1 through D+1.

**Why the enumeration covers all distances.** Fill a finite set of black positions in increasing order, with whites everywhere else. Once that row fails at d, any additional black cell that can change its failure must occur within bits 0 through d. Branch on the next black cell at each still available position in this range. Every extension whose next black cell is farther away inherits the failure. This partitions all rows by their first potentially relevant added black site. An extension can still have an *earlier* failure. Each new row is therefore tested from tick zero. Ending a branch at its first failure preserves the upper bound because the light cone already fixes a white reading. Each maximizing finite row establishes sharpness separately. [Frozen adaptive search](sparse_wall.py)

**Independent computation.** The second program grows the initial prefix bit by bit. Upon appending bit j it computes the diagonal ending at the wall at tick j by cumulative XOR along the previous two diagonals, using the physical zero beyond the wall. At even j, a white wall observation discards the whole prefix. It found zero survivors at tick 132 and agreed at every budget on the deadline and the population, 4,684,155 nonempty failure prefixes in total. The two traversals choose different maximizing witnesses at four intermediate weights. The general verifier, the universal eight-black upper bound, and the sharp lower witnesses at 8, 17 and 18 passed core Lean. The 17- and 18-black upper bounds are independently exhaustive computations. AIOvernight's Sparse16 already kernel-certified the 16/111/110 result, with Patto1155 and Placego credited for smaller sparse populations under their own conventions. ## What must go to infinity?

**Weight.** For every budget b, ask for a finite H(b) such that any half-line passing ticks 0,2,…,2H has more than b initial black cells among depths 1,…,2H+1. In core Lean this condition is equivalent to Q01. From Q01 each explicit finite row reaches a first failed check. Each node then has finitely many relevant children and each child spends a black cell. Induction on the remaining budget gives a common finite deadline. Conversely a finite row has some total budget and must fail at its deadline. The kit already uses weight-budget compactness for kick languages in Rule30ZeroCoastBridge §13. Here the predicate is the bare even-time wall and the verifier branches on physical black sites. The equivalence requires no linear rate. The missing step is a proof of deadlines for all budgets independent of assuming Q01. [`q01_iff_prefix_floors`](SparseDeadline.lean)

**Conditional check product.** In any fixed finite population of post-support survivors of size A₀>0, write Aⱼ for the number still passing after j more checks. The product of successive conditional fractions is Aⱼ/A₀ until extinction, after which the surviving fraction is zero. At this fixed width the integer count makes convergence to zero equivalent to eventual extinction. A decaying upper bound valid at every horizon for each finite width would suffice. Multiplying the unconditioned individual check probabilities gives a different quantity. At widths 44 and 48 the measured eleventh conditional fraction falls on opposite sides of 1/2. Likewise the measured weight slopes depend on the demand language, with about 0.2486 for GE2 and about 0.275 in an earlier finite trap census. An unbounded floor on physically served kick-anchored histories would exclude an eventual alternating singleton; a bare west row may never be served. Q01 concerns all bare west rows, and it excludes period two. Other positive periods require additional arguments. [Measured checks](../ladder/free-cube-results.md) · [W1 quantifiers](../../../WALLS.md)

**Count distinct evolved rows at any age.** Let E(n,k,h) count the distinct tick-h images of starts below 4ⁿ passing k even checks starting at zero. An immortal y would give at least n−⌊log₂(y)/2⌋ distinct images for every n,k,h, with truncation at zero. Use the even iterates of the same y as starting rows. Their leading black cells lie two sites apart and all advance one site per tick. This separation persists however late the images are read. Under Q01 each fixed-width census eventually empties. Hence Q01 is equivalent to arbitrarily large n−E, even with a prescribed age h(n,k). For K92.2, **an unbounded deficit with k=2n, h=4n suffices**; fixing k too is only the sufficient direction. I317's ceiling of 4,096 preimages per 24-tick endpoint was used to bound the number of starts. Counting images directly needs no growing-age multiplicity assumption. A proof that enough images disappear at arbitrarily large widths is still missing. [AgedCensus](AgedCensus.lean) · [Earlier finite-fibre argument](../borrowed/arithmetic-transfer.md)
