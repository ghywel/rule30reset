# An isolated black cell within seven wheels after a first clock loss in Rule 30

## Abstract

On a recurrent Rule 30 left-edge strip, let wheel `a` be permanently white, let the next wheel first lose the clock of period `2^e`, and let `b` be the next permanently white wheel. In every five consecutive ticks, three successive wheels between `a` and `min(b,a+7)` read white, black, white. Of any three consecutive ticks and their translates by `2^e`, one has such a singleton by `min(b,a+6)`. On a strip pointed by a black edge, one fixed tick of any five sees these singletons at arbitrarily deep first losses. All three statements hold for every clock exponent and every starting tick. Quint and Gemini proved the post-loss cascade, a catch after each driver fall, catches for low clock orders, and catches after long constant runs. Their results together give a reset in fifteen ticks within nine wheels. The present proof uses short driver runs to guarantee an isolated black within five ticks. It leaves open whether the prescribed tick in `DLMinusOne` must succeed.

**Lean headlines** are `ClockPhasePairs.five_ticks`, `ClockPhasePairs.three_pairs` and `ClockPhasePairs.cofinal_singletons`.

## Essay draft

A wheel is a column in the coordinates that follow the left edge of Rule 30. A finite prefix of wheels has a common time period. The first loss of a clock of period `P=2^e` occurs at a driver wheel whose reading changes to its complement after `P` ticks. We use the permanently white wheel `a` immediately before that driver and the next permanently white wheel `b`. A *singleton* is the whole spatial word `010`, including both white neighbours. Each occurrence gives a reset, since the enclosed run has length one.

Quint and Gemini proved that wheel `a+2` is black throughout and that `a+3` reads the complement of the previous driver bit. A fall of the driver from black to white therefore gives `010` at the ending tick. They also showed that nine consecutive white driver bits force a reset by `a+9`, and eight black bits force one by `a+8`, at the respective run endpoints. Together these imply a reset in any fifteen consecutive ticks. Include the tick immediately before that window. Either the sixteen bits have a fall, or they consist of whites followed by blacks. In the latter case there are at least nine initial whites or eight final blacks. Prefix periodicity permits the same argument for a window beginning at zero. A reset here can enclose a black run of length `1 mod 3`. [Quint and Gemini's cascade](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30RelaxedWindows.lean) · [Their run captures](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/ea5d70e/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30FirstLossBounds.lean)

Two successive black driver readings keep `a+3` white while `a+4` toggles. At one of the two updates the toggling wheel falls. If its next neighbour is white, its last black cell is already isolated. If that neighbour is black, the recurrence gives a singleton one tick later and one wheel farther out. Three successive white driver readings give the same argument one wheel farther out again. The driver's antiperiod ensures that the intervening wheel is black at some time and so lies before the next permanently white wheel. Four driver bits have a fall, start with three whites, or end in two blacks. These three cases give the five-tick bound. Comparing the driver with its complement at displacement `P` gives the six tested ticks of the second theorem. For the third, take first losses at arbitrarily large depths. If each of the five ticks saw its last such singleton at a finite depth, a first loss beyond all five would contradict the first theorem.

Quint and Gemini already give a catch on every prescribed tick for `e ≤ 4`. The five-tick bound is uniform into `e ≥ 5`, the remaining clock orders for the doubling question. On configurations with a leftmost black cell, the four physical left sides have period 16 through wheel 87,866. The higher-order first losses lie beyond that verified prefix. The prescribed-tick question asks for a catch on one of the two specified phases. Quint and Gemini's `local_paired_nfa_escapes_in_five` exhibits the limitation of a relaxed paired-window test. Our theorem allows the tick to move within the stated window.
