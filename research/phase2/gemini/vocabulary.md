# Q4 · The singleton's western vocabulary, including late ages

**Proved in core Lean.** On the actual single seed, for every k ≥ 0 and T ≥ k+1, at least k+2 different spatial words on the fixed strip [−(2k+1),0] occur at ages in **[T, 2T+k+1]**, endpoints included. There are also k+2 fixed words on this strip each occurring at arbitrarily late ages. For instance every T ≥ 40 gives at least 41 different 80-cell snapshots by age 2T+40. In the kit’s width-w convention (w+1 cells), Galindo already gives w+⌊w/39⌋+1 words by age w+3⌊w/39⌋. His theorem certifies first visits near the initial edge crossing. This result supplies a deadline that can be restarted arbitrarily late. ## What would settle Gemini's proposed inference?

Galindo's H(w) counts the **w+1** cells at sites 0,−1,…,−w over all singleton ages. If the centre repeats with period P after T, he proves H(w) ≤ T+P⌊9(39/32)ʷ⌋. Thus H(w)/(39/32)ʷ unbounded would give Problem 1. Quadratic growth would be a new unconditional rung. A quadratic lower bound is numerically compatible with that periodic upper bound for arbitrarily large w. Even a lower bound with exponential base strictly between 1 and 39/32 would be compatible with it. Problem 2 asks for the time frequency of one bit. H counts each word once irrespective of the number of visits. The kit's bound by itself provides no frequency estimate. ## Why the late words must occur

1. Take the two middle sites i = −k−1 and i+1. Equality of two initial snapshots on the strip makes this pair equal for k+1 successive ticks, by the radius-one light cone.
2. Inspect the first k+2 ages starting at T. If their snapshots are distinct the count is complete. Otherwise let a<b≤T+k+1 have equal snapshots. The pair traces agree for at least k+1 ticks. By the kit's finite-left masked Jen theorem, they disagree by offset b+i = b−k−1. Agreement beyond that would reconstruct the moving western edge at the same site at ages when it has different colours.
3. Let q be their first disagreement. For every j = 0,…,k, the two length-j pair words starting at a+q−j and b+q−j agree, and their next letters differ. The starts are at most b+q ≤ 2T+k+1. Each such branching length-j word forces at least one extra distinguishable length-(j+1) word on the same set of permitted start ages. To see this, take a list with different length-j prefixes. At most one representative has the branching prefix, so at least one of its two continuations can be added. Induction starting with the empty prefix gives k+2 length-(k+1) pair words. The light-cone observation lifts them to k+2 spatial snapshots.
4. Only finitely many spatial words of a fixed width exist. Beyond the last transient occurrence, every word still seen recurs cofinally. Choosing T beyond this cutoff gives k+2 fixed cofinal words. The finite-window witnesses themselves may depend on T.

The branching-prefix argument is the one behind Morse–Hedlund factor complexity. Here it acts on the **temporal trace of a pair**. Equality of spatial snapshots supplies a long repeated trace segment, and the kit supplies a finite bound on its first disagreement. No shift-closure of the anchored horizontal vocabulary is assumed. The Lean theorem also treats any row with leftmost black lo and every strip [i−k,i+1+k], with T+i ≥ lo and deadline T+k+1+(T+k+1+i−lo). The eastern half may be infinite. ## A sufficient test for a superlinear rung

Use the exact disjoint-pair census of Galindo. At terminal width s, each depth r<s gets a collection of age pairs. Both ages agree on the first s cells, differ on the next cell and have matching future centre readings for r ticks; their translated endpoints lie among the N observation ages. The pairs are endpoint-disjoint separately at each depth. Let J be the sum of the numbers of pairs over the depths. The published bound is 4ᴶ ≤ H(s)ᴺ. At dyadic width s = 2ʲ, **J ≥ jN gives H(s) ≥ s²**. This follows by raising the target to the Nth power. S22's density 1/7 across all age-depth positions is sufficient for Problem 1. Here the required density is only j/2ʲ for a quadratic lower bound. The existence of these censuses for unbounded j is open. This provides an alternative to the existing I295.4 test on the total number of repeated snapshots. ## A finite-seed control for the density inference

**Hand construction.** Consider a translation-invariant radius-one automaton with one permanent marker state and four data states (bit, carry). Start from one marker at 0 with (0,0) elsewhere. A marker sends a carry west every tick. A data site's incoming bit u is the carry sent by its eastern neighbour; if its own data bit is b, its new state is (b xor u, b and u). The all-zero background is quiescent.

The j-th site west of the marker emits a carry at ticks (j−1)+m·2ʲ for integers m ≥ 1. For j=1 these are the positive even ticks. The next site toggles once per incoming pulse and emits a pulse on every second toggle, one tick later, which proves the formula by induction. Its data bit at tick t ≥ j−1 is bit j−1 of t−j+1. For any w, the w data bits distinguish all residues modulo 2ʷ at t ≥ w. If two residues first differ at binary place j−1, they still differ at that place after subtracting j−1 since their lower bits agree. Thus the observed west bits realize every w-bit pattern cofinally. The marker's own state stays constant and its chosen black-indicator frequency is one. This control uses a five-state rule. It isolates why a dynamics-specific bridge is needed to constrain Rule 30's one-site frequency from a spatial vocabulary.

**Verdict.** The unconditional advance is a linear bound on late and recurrent vocabulary with a finite observation deadline. The dyadic quadratic test is a conditional route to superlinearity. A first disagreement yields one branch per prefix length; obtaining many distinct branching *prefixes* at those lengths, or the terminal-census condition above, remains the missing step.
