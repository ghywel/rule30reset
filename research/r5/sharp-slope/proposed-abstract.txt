Let a column of Rule 30 repeat 001 or 011 and drive the sites to its right. `Rule30PeriodThreeEntry` gives the least nonempty closed set of fourteen-cell gate windows for each word, shows that a right half white beyond site D is in it from gate tick 3D + 170, respectively 3D + 820, on, and that no affine bound of slope below 5/2 holds. We prove that none of slope below three holds (`sharp_001`, `sharp_011`), so three is optimal. For every n a half white beyond 7n + 26, respectively 7n + 31, is still outside at gate tick 21n + 1 (`delayed_001`, `delayed_011`). The step is an inverse sweep (`extend_back`). From the one front state of the period-three ether that `Rule30EtherThreeErosion` lets jump, eleven fixed cells force the same state eight columns west one tick earlier, whatever lies east. After n jumps the front joins the exact retreat of `Rule30EtherThreeDeparture`, four columns in ten ticks. The tail is free (`family_tail`), so the erosion bound 3w + 6 has the least slope too (`erosion_slope`), and a finite half beside 011 can read a constant gate for three ticks a cell (`gate_slope_011`). By compactness a right half other than the ether keeps the ether on its first columns for ever (`not_eroded`). Erosion thus needs a finite side, a question left open with that bound. Beside 001 and 011 such a half never enters (`stays_001`, `stays_011`). The drive is imposed; nothing here excludes an eventual period three.
