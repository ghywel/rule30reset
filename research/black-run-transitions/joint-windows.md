# Sharp two-run windows and the joint period exclusion

I93 continuation by gptIntel, 26 November 2026. Black is 1 and white is 0. The window $1^a0\,1^r$ ends immediately before the sampled tick. For each positive $r$, the first right neighbour is forced to a common colour for all occurrences if and only if $a$ reaches the threshold for $r$. The upper bound applies at any site and starting time.

| Last black run $r$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | ≥9 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Least preceding black run $a$ | 8 | 8 | 8 | 15 | 8 | 16 | 4 | 14 | 0 |
| Forced right neighbour | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The upper bound uses I95's seven-cell strip for the eight short runs. A suffix of nine blacks supplies the last column. Below a threshold, both readings occur at any prescribed site even if the initial row must have finite support and a leftmost black cell. Start from the two old cone witnesses at $a=\text{threshold}-1$ and begin observing later to shorten $a$. Copy just the cells in the observation cones to a finite row. Place a single extra black immediately to the west of that interval so that each witness has a leftmost black cell. Translating the finite row puts the observations at the requested site. These are local sharpness witnesses over finite rows. The witness seed can depend on the requested colour and window. [Lean proof](Rule30RunTransitionWindows.lean)

## Each white phase can use its own rule

Kurtuluş's five-cell strip checks constrain the first right cell or the OR of the first two. His black-run parity step propagates these facts through an arbitrarily long run of blacks. Both steps work at any site of any full row. Use his windows and the sharp pair windows together. At a white phase $j$ of a nonempty cyclic word, it is enough to find either a kit window or an I93 pair window ending immediately before $j$. Different phases can choose different rules. The kernel theorem `not_eventually_combined` then excludes the word at every integer site on every row with a leftmost black cell, from any transient. Its more general form `exclude_of_windows` accepts any bounded choice of local windows with proofs of their forced colours.

For the argument, take a late white tick and another one period later. They have the same phase, so the same certificate fixes the same right reading at both. Finite-left masked Jen rules this out once all white phases have such a certificate. The bounded window length ensures the comparisons stay inside the periodic tail, including windows which straddle a period boundary. This applies to any number of white ticks and does not impose isolated whites everywhere. [Kurtuluş's original window theorem](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/71151d0/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30RunTransitions.lean) · [PR #381](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/pull/381)

## A primitive family that needs both rules

Let one white follow each entry of the cyclic run schedule $(16,4,9,1,4,9)$, with zero or more additional 9s appended. The word has length $49+10n$. Every run of length at least nine has the kit's nine-black suffix. The 4 after 16 uses I93's preceding threshold 15. The 1 after 9 uses the kit's eight-black prefix and odd continuation, and the 4 after 1 uses Kurtuluş's `b81` and even continuation. The two critical windows are internal to the first 39 ticks, so appending any number of nines changes neither.

At the first 4, the kit's three short terminal suffixes and all of its odd continuations fail. The even continuation would have to use all four final blacks, with a preceding one-black run. The actual preceding run is 16. At the second 4 the preceding run is 1, below I95's threshold of 15. These two failures also hold after any appended nines, since the entire preceding run and its ending white are in the fixed part. There is exactly one maximal 16-black run on the cycle. A nontrivial cyclic period would move its start to another occurrence, which proves the word is primitive. This supplies infinitely many examples with the same two failures. Lean checks the general exclusion and the 49-tick separating example. The family and primitivity arguments in this paragraph are in prose. An independent script also checks the first 17 members. ## Relation to the existing exclusions

- Kurtuluş's `Rule30RunTransitions` gives the original phase test for the singleton centre, with run patterns certified on unrestricted five-cell strips. The extension keeps every original window, permits a per-phase mixture with I95, and lifts the period consequence and sharp local witnesses to every site of finite-left rows. His `b81` rule is essential at the second 4 in the example. [Original theorem](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/pull/381)
- PR #416 checks 324 primitive words of lengths 14 through 18 using the kit's strip certificate checker. Its maintainer comment calls for a general rule or infinite family. The joint theorem uses the same eight short-run cutoffs and the kit's windows at every period length. [PR #416 and maintainer discussion](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/pull/416)
- Dyer's classification in the attached notebook for R30-20261124-cacfbd concerns repetitions of one black run and one white run, uniformly over all finite-left sites. His B3 result treats a run of three blacks and at least fifteen whites. The mixed primitive words here contain several black runs separated by single whites. The nearby kit modules cover long white runs, quarter-period runs, and particular strip certificates. These differ in hypotheses from the joint local forcing theorem. This comparison does not claim that no separate certificate can exclude a particular word in the new family.
