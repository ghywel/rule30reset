# Black-run transitions and the right-hand shield

Katip Kurtuluş’s open PR 381 excludes periodic singleton-center words when the runs before each white tick satisfy a local condition. At head `a9dc16e`, the stock checker passed its main theorem and replayed 124 declarations. Two extensions use the same observation, the color one cell to the right at a white tick. The local result below is sharp for ordinary configurations. The shield result improves the kit’s bound at arbitrarily large widths. [PR 381](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/pull/381) · [Kit black-run theorem](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/40017e8/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30BlackRuns.lean).

## Exactly what two consecutive black runs determine

Prescribe the column word **1ᵃ 0 1ʳ**, where a ≥ 0 and r ≥ 1. At the very next tick, the first neighbor on the right has a common color for *every ordinary initial configuration* exactly when a reaches the threshold below. No assumption on the preceding history, the exterior, or the next center bit is needed.

| Final black run r | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | ≥9 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Least preceding run a | 8 | 8 | 8 | **15** | 8 | **16** | 4 | **14** | 0 |
| Forced right color | white | black | white | black | white | black | white | white | white |

A width-seven strip, allowing either exterior bit at every tick, proves the eight short-run upper bounds. The last nine black ticks alone suffice for the long-run column. Each of the eight finite thresholds has two ordinary witnesses one tick earlier with opposite readings. Starting those histories later realizes both colors for each shorter preceding run. The Lean theorem `exact_pair` states both directions. The accompanying scalar replay constructs finite left halves and evolves the resulting finite rows freely. [Reproduce the witnesses and upper bounds](check-run-transitions.py).

Now put one white tick between successive positive black runs. On a row with a leftmost black cell, no site can eventually repeat a run schedule in which every run meets the table’s condition with its cyclic predecessor. The forced right color at every white tick would then repeat with the drive, contradicting finite-left masked Jen. This covers arbitrary transient, phase and period. In particular, any short run may follow a run of at least 16, as long as the condition holds at every other transition too. The cyclic schedule (16,4,17,6,18,8) passes this test and fails PR 381’s condition. The kernel theorem is `pair_clock_exclusion`. [Finite-left masked Jen](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/40017e8/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30FiniteMaskedJen.lean).

The previous width-five repeated-110 result genuinely enlarges the set of *local* forcing contexts. On an entire periodic schedule with isolated whites, however, **the exact width-five and width-six readers accept precisely PR 381’s schedules** when acceptance requires an eventually definite reading at every white tick. Here the reader tracks all possible rows, allowing either right exterior bit each tick. In the reachable state product with the previous two run lengths, every PR-good transition already gives a definite reading. Along a path of definite readings, at most two transitions can miss the PR condition. The finite integer ranks have maximum two at both widths. A periodically recurring missed condition would be visited infinitely often. The independent checker reconstructs all 167 and 290 unminimized product states respectively. The restriction to isolated whites matters. [Reproduce the comparison](check-run-transitions.py).

A different extension permits consecutive whites. Under arbitrary concatenation of blocks 1⁸ 0ᵐ 1⁴ 0 1 0 with m ≥ 9, the late right reading at a white center tick t is determined by the two center bits at t−1 and t−5: it equals ¬c(t−1) ∨ ¬c(t−5). Both lags together suffice, and neither any single lag nor the four preceding ticks suffice for a uniform decoder on ordinary histories. Consequently no eventual periodic singleton center can consist of these blocks. The read-back packets `I83/pr381-mixed-pads.zip` and `I83/mixed-pad-decoder.zip` contain the kernel theorems and lower witnesses.

## A faster black-run shield

The merged kit proves that 9j consecutive black ticks fix the next 2j right cells to (white, black)ʲ. Three sharp finite cones improve its rate.

| Right cells pinned | Prior kit guarantee | Exact black ticks required | Last failing tick |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 45 | 44 | 43 |
| 16 | 72 | 70 | 69 |
| 30 | 135 | **128** | 127 |

Each upper certificate starts with every right bit free and demands a discrepancy in the terminal row. The triangular cone enforces the eight Rule 30 truth-table clauses for each updated cell. Both cake_lpr and lrat-check validated the three UNSAT proofs. For the 30-cell result the instance has 12,127 variables and 95,746 clauses. For each lower bound an explicit right seed yields a discrepant terminal row in direct scalar evolution. Left permutivity realizes the prescribed black run on a full line. The width-18 open-strip computation independently proves the 44-tick upper result with arbitrary boundary refreshes.

**For every j, 9j − 7⌊j/15⌋ consecutive black ticks suffice to fix the first 2j right cells.** Use the 128-tick shield for each group of fifteen pairs and the kit’s nine-tick bound for the remainder. To compose two shields, slide the first certificate along the still-black input. Its rightmost even cell stays black throughout the second certificate’s required interval, so that cell drives the next group. Applying the first certificate to the final portion of the interval also retains the earlier pairs at the endpoint. The 44- and 70-tick certificates can further improve some remainders. This gives an upper bound for general j. Exactness is asserted for the three measured widths. [Original finite-configuration shield](https://github.com/WolframInstitute/Rule30PrizeKit/blob/40017e8/lean/Ruliology/CellularAutomata/Rule30FiniteBlackRuns.lean).

There is a direct period consequence. In the kit’s self-shielded quarter-period exclusion, let j = ⌈(P−r)/4⌉ for a period P containing r consecutive black ticks followed by white. Its required shield time 9j can be replaced by **9j − 7⌊j/15⌋**. For example the new test excludes r = 128 at P = 188, where that kit test required 135. The rest of the agreement and masked-Jen argument uses the alternating prefix at the end of the run.

The finite cone refutations have externally checked LRAT certificates. The Lean composition argument and its connection to physical rows are separate. Direct `bv_decide` used a native-evaluation axiom, so its output cannot serve as a stock kernel-only certificate. The independent finite-automaton route has 44 transitions for the smallest improved shield. Its generic local update proof compiles. Two of the 44 numerical transitions have completed kernel checking. A 16-case block from the third transition also passed. The remaining work is to combine these smaller blocks under the host memory limit.
