Troy Lowry proved that 80 positive cells from the single seed at age 364 force entry into the width-30 seven-phase lock and give a sharp seven-site moving-edge disagreement. His construction motivates a criterion for every finite right prefix under a black-first alternating centre. Every infinite completion eventually enters the lock if and only if a finite binary certificate passes. Each leaf specifies an entry time and phase. Finite dependence and compactness give completeness even when these vary across completions. A two-leaf tree certifies entry at tick one for a 29-cell prefix. One completion of its first 28 cells avoids the lock forever. Using Lowry’s periodic ring, we show that a certified prefix at actual singleton age A forces a disagreement with alternation by absolute tick 2(A + E), where E is the largest certified entry tick. Arbitrarily late such occurrences at black-centre ages would exclude eventual period two. Their occurrence remains open.
