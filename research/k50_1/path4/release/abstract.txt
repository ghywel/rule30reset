Every infinite completion of a finite right prefix enters the width-30 seven-phase lock under a black-first alternating centre if and only if a finite binary certificate passes (`Rule30Submission.eventual_iff`). Troy Lowry’s age-364 construction led to this criterion. He proved entry from 80 positive cells and a sharp seven-site moving-edge disagreement. The kit’s `Rule30SidewaysLocks` supplies the lock and its closure. We use the diagonal argument of `Rule30ResetCompactness` to obtain a common deadline when entry times and phases vary. Each certificate leaf specifies its entry tick and phase. A two-leaf tree gives entry at tick one for a 29-cell prefix. One completion of its first 28 cells avoids the lock forever. Using Lowry’s periodic ring, we prove that a certified prefix at singleton age A forces a centre disagreement by absolute tick 2(A + E), where E is the latest certified entry tick. Arbitrarily late occurrences at black-centre ages would exclude eventual period two. Establishing those occurrences remains open.
