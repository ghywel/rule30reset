# A phase can miss every white-diagonal drop

**R37.** There is a black-edged recurrent Rule 30 strip with a single phase in which the cell immediately beyond every permanently white diagonal is black. A drop at phase zero would have to end in white there. This rules out M39's proposed universal source of drops. Reset words elsewhere in the row can still close inverse histories.

### One phase at all widths

Write $D_j(t)$ for wheel $j$ at time $t$, starting at the black edge $D_0=D_1=1$. Suppose two columns $a,b$ share a period $P$ from tick zero. The next column has two possible initial bits and evolves by $c(t+1)=a(t)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(b(t)\lor c(t))$. The map taking its initial bit through one period is a self-map of a two-element set. Some initial bit returns after two periods. If $b$ is identically white, each update is an XOR with $a(t)$. The period map is then identity or complementation, so **black** always returns after two periods. Choose it. Repeat at every depth, keeping the same time origin. Full prefixes have common periods, even when individual columns double their periods.

The [Lean proof](Rule30PointedWhiteFork.lean) uses the kit's `Recurrence` and `PeriodFrom`. The theorem `exists_pointed_strip` gives the simultaneous choice and a common period $2^w$ for wheels 0 through $w$. The companion `finite_seed_prefix` truncates the time-zero row to those wheels. Induction on time using the triangular recurrence proves that its finite seed reproduces the prefix forever. This realizes the configurations used in the compactness argument. A different width can require a different seed.

The white anchors themselves recur (M39, carrying read). Wheel 2 is white. If there were a last white wheel, its two successors have some common period $P$. Every subsequent nonwhite driver has a black tick which fixes its child's next bit independently of its old value. Periodicity of the child from the common origin at some period lets us propagate the resulting $P$-periodicity back to that origin. Inductively the whole tail has $P$-periodic columns. Two consecutive-column pairs must repeat strictly beyond the last white. The backward spatial rule $a(t)=c(t+1)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(b(t)\lor c(t))$ is single-valued, so the repetition propagates back to a later copy of the last white, a contradiction. This paragraph is a hand argument. The Lean counterexample itself needs no recurrence-of-whites assumption.
