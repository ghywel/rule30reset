Thank you for asking what the ages 8, 24 and 80 measure. The first two are sharp thresholds. The 80 is a sufficient age from our four-tick cover. Starting a different cover from the seed’s top eight cells proves that age 72 already suffices. A seed still fails at age 58, so the sharp four-tick threshold lies between 59 and 72. At three ticks we can pin it down exactly to 31.

**What makes a cover close.** Read a row from the fixed white wall towards its outer edge. Each backward layer keeps two adjacent parent cells. At age at least $k$, the actual orbit supplies one finite history. If all $2^k$ possible wall histories agree on their full register at some cut, they agree from there to the edge and all close. Conversely, if all are finite, their registers are all zero at the edge. Forward evolution moves a nonzero finite row’s edge one cell per tick, so a finite $k$-tick predecessor has its edge exactly $k$ cells inside the image edge.

The top $w$ cells evolve autonomously. At width 20 their eventual cycle is C20, the four-word base of the Lean proof. This separates two costs in the certificate. Let $K(w)$ be the greatest number of inverse layers that every phase of a converged width-$w$ edge band clears, even when every incoming register is allowed. Let $g(w)$ be an age by which our moving cover certifies that every seed’s top $w$ cells have reached that cycle. Whenever $K(w)\ge k$, we obtain $f(k)\le g(w)$.

For example, in the widths checked, $K(w)=3$ from 25 through 61. The fourth layer first clears at width 62. The converged-band test gives ages 34 and 93 at those two widths, while the seed-anchored cover gives 31 and 72. This is why a further layer can cost many ticks in a certificate, and why changing the cover can recover some of them. At widths 20–128 the converged band gained nine layers over 108 additional cells. The certified convergence ages were 26 at width 20, 97 at width 64 and 178 at width 128. For five and six ticks the sharp ages lie in 73–78 and 104–108 respectively. For seven through eleven ticks our upper bounds are 131, 132, 144, 157 and 172. The sharp thresholds need not follow those finite-range rates.

**Progress towards arbitrary depth.** At width 4,096 we checked all 16 phases using only the known one-layer reset pattern: a zero, then $3m+1$ ones, then another zero and one further bit. Once all four incoming pairs agree, the next layer can read the common suffix of the preceding phase. This schedule certifies 9 layers at width 128, 94 at 1,024 and **383 at 4,096**, in the worst phase. At width 4,096, every start with at least 65 cells available before the edge synchronised within 65 cells. Of 65,536 phase/start pairs, 96 ran out of band before synchronising.

The kit’s `Rule30UniversalLeftSide` identifies the eventual strip for every leftmost-black configuration through wheel 53,207. This includes the entire width-4,096 band. Once a finite row spans that width, its band evolves autonomously on at most $2^{4096}$ words. Together these facts give the coarse uniform age $4095+2^{4096}$ for every $k\le383$. The new finite calculations have been independently replayed. The 383-layer result is computer-assisted and its Lean formalisation remains open. **The open step** is to prove that for every $k$ some width clears $k$ layers on every reachable eventual edge cycle, including beyond the first strip fork. A uniform bound on the distance to the next one-layer reset would be one way to do that. The 65-cell observation gives us a concrete recurrence property to investigate. A finite convergence time would then suffice for the existence of $f(k)$; a linear rate is a stronger question. This is the step we are pursuing towards the all-depth statement you requested.
