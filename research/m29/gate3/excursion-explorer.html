Rule 30 Excursion Explorer

A doubled excursion of rule 30's left side: the stretch of wheels after an always-white wheel where the clock doubles. Choose or type a source, and see every row of one period, its reset words, what made them, and where each row is first caught.

What you are looking at

Reset word. White, then 1, 4, 7, … black cells, then white. It matters when a row is read backwards in time. Someone who knows a row but not the row above it can work the row above out cell by cell, from right to left, but only by guessing the first two cells. There are four guesses. On passing a reset word all four give the same answer, and from there to the left the row above is certain.

Caught. A row is caught when it has a reset word somewhere in the stretch. Caught at wheel 23 means that, counting from the left end, its first reset word finishes at wheel 23.

Slow row. A row that is caught late, far to the right. A stretch is built from left to right, each wheel from the two before it, and it ends where a wheel comes out white at every tick. A slow row would have had no reset word if the stretch had ended sooner. Never caught means the stretch did end first.

What the marks mean

  • A reset word is a run of 1, 4, 7, … black cells with a white cell on each side. A row is caught once it holds one.
  • A single black cell between two whites can only be made by four patterns in the row above: 11000, 10110, 10111, 10101.
  • 11000 is a black run followed by three whites: the right corner of the white triangle under that run. 1011 is black, white, then a run: the left corner of the triangle under the next run.
  • A white triangle hangs under every black run. Its right side stands still and its left side moves two wheels a tick, because wheels are counted from the moving edge.
  • The strip on the far left is the source, the wheel before wheel 0. Each black tick there flips the colour of wheel 1.

Things to try

  • See what a reset word does. In period 5, click the right-hand end of row 5: the four guesses agree from wheel 17 leftwards. Then click the right-hand end of row 1, which is framed: they never agree. Then click row 5 again at wheel 15, to the left of its reset word: they never agree either.
  • Period 5, one black tick. The only doubled excursion known in which rows are never caught. Every reset word in it is a single cell.
  • One black tick at period 40. Between flips the pattern settles into the ordinary left side; only the few rows after a flip are slow, and all are caught by wheel 20.
  • The single black cell's own intervals. Its sources are busy ones: 1, 01, 1101, 00101100.
  • Three black ticks, gaps 5, 18, 27. The slowest three-tick source there is: its last row is caught at wheel 84. Reverse the gaps and it is 21.
  • Type your own. The number of black ticks per period must be odd, or the clock does not double.
