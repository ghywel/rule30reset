## K2–K5 · What determines the next white wheel?

**K2's two structural steps pass.** Write the three adjacent temporal profiles as $a,b,c$, all with a common positive period $Q$. At a black tick of the middle wheel, the recurrence fixes the child's next bit to the complement of the farther wheel's bit. Iteration around the period fixes the entire child. Every interior driver in a whole white interval has such a tick. Thus the left white wheel and its chosen first child determine the interval up to its next white. The kit gives existence and uniqueness in `Rule30TemporalProfiles.child_exists` and `child_unique`.

If the child is identically white, substitution in the recurrence gives $a=b$ at all ticks. Conversely, when $a=b$, the zero word solves the recurrence, and uniqueness makes it the actual periodic child. The nonzero-driver hypothesis matters here. The kit's direct reference is `Rule30LeftCoincidence.evwhite_iff_eventually_eq`, with periodicity carrying eventual equality back to every phase. **The probability step remains a conjecture.** Adjacent words have a common deterministic history. An equality probability of $2^{-Q}$ requires a distributional argument. A geometric waiting time further requires control of the return probability conditional on having survived to each depth. Even a correct mean cannot exclude a single early orbit. For a uniform doubling lemma one needs control of early return and late reset **on the same orbit**. Bounds on the latest reset and earliest return over all sources would suffice. A direct comparison within each orbit would also suffice.

**A deterministic part of the counting picture (PROOF-SKETCH, for a second read).** Fix a positive clock length $Q$ and average over **all** $2^Q-1$ nonzero first-child words $c$ at a white anchor, each pointed word once. Let $g(c)$ be the next white’s distance. The pairs of successive columns with nonzero second member on this excursion number $g(c)-1$, starting with $(0,c)$. An interior pair $(b,d)$ uniquely recovers its predecessor $(a,b)$ by $a(t)=d(t+1)\mathbin{\mathrm{xor}}(b(t)\lor d(t))$. Tracing back to the first zero left member therefore recovers both $c$ and the distance travelled. No pair repeats, either within or between these excursions. There are only $2^Q(2^Q-1)$ pairs with a nonzero second member, so the **pooled mean gap is at most $2^Q+1$**. This uses the same backward uniqueness as the kit’s `rooted_chain_returns_bounded`. The sampling weights differ from K3’s exact-period, parity-conditioned samples. A high mean, a constant conditional return probability and a lower bound on the earliest doubling each still need another argument.
