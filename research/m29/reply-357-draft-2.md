Following up on the statement for every $k$. We now have it in Lean by one argument for every $k$, conditional on one named property of the edge band, which is still open. It is posted as a partial result: [{ENTRY}]({ENTRY_PAGE}).

**The theorem** (`conditional_every_k`). Read the top $w$ cells of a row from its edge. They evolve on their own, and settle on a cycle of that band map. Let $K_1(w)$ be the number of inverse layers that a fixed schedule clears on every phase of every cycle at width $w$. If for every $k$ some width has $K_1(w)\ge k$, then for every $k$, from age $w-1+2^w$ with $w$ the least such width, every $k$-tick preimage of a nonzero finite orbit is finite with its true edge $k$ cells inside the image edge, and all $2^k$ choices of cell 1 at times $0,\dots,k-1$ occur. The proof is one induction over the layers, with no cover for any $k$. A sharper form takes any proved settling age for the band in place of $w-1+2^w$. As a control, kernel computation of the capacity at widths 8 and 20 recovers the one- and two-tick statements without the hypothesis, from ages 263 and 1,048,595.

**The schedule.** A closed run is $0\,1^{3m+1}\,0$, and a complete factor is a closed run followed by one more cell $z$. One inverse layer, started with all four registers allowed, has the single register (black, not $z$) once it has read a complete factor, with $z$ read once. This is the reset step of #60 (`run_resets`), and the closing of a scheduled cascade of any height is its `scheduled_cascade_closes`. #60 is unmerged, so it is not in the verifier's kit revision; our module proves both steps locally, with credit to Troy Lowry, and a kit version would import them once #60 is merged. Each later layer starts where the last one coalesced, and reads the cycle's phase one tick earlier. Those phases agree with every preimage on the bits already fixed, so no extra width is needed for the true ancestors to settle.

**Why the ages grow.** The theorem gives $f(k)\le g(W(k))$. Here $W(k)$ is the least width whose capacity reaches $k$, and $g(w)$ is the age by which every width-$w$ band has settled. Both grow linearly where we can compute them. The capacity rises by one layer per 10.7 to 11.1 wheels, exactly computed from width 4,096 to 87,867, and the bands settle in about 1.4 ticks per wheel, certified up to width 128. Their product, a heuristic since the two rates come from different widths, is about 15 ticks of age per further tick. The known ages, 8, 24 and 31 for one to three ticks, 59–72 for four and at most 172 for eleven, rise at about that rate. No single age can serve every $k$: closing every $k$-tick history of the single black cell at age $t$ needs $t\ge 2k$.

**What we know of the hypothesis.**
- It holds for every $k\le 7{,}911$. The kit's `forced_front_four_uniform` and an exact computation on every phase of the four strips give $K_1=7{,}911$ at width 87,867. With the kit's explicit fronts as the settling age, this gives $f(k)\le 265{,}441$ for every $k\le 7{,}911$. This part is computer-assisted and outside Lean.
- It follows, by compactness, from every phase of every recurrent infinite strip having closed runs at arbitrarily large depths. These strips are the infinite left sides whose finite prefixes are the band cycles. That condition is also necessary for this route. Read on the odd 2-adic integers with the band map $x\mapsto 4x\oplus(2x\vee x)$, it says that every recurrent point has exactly one preimage.
- Our last comment suggested a uniform bound on the distance to the next reset. The data say that is the wrong target: the longest reset-free stretch keeps growing with depth, to 87 cells by wheel 87,866, much as the longest run in coin flips does. Infinitely many resets in every phase are enough.
- In Lean, one fixed phase in any three consecutive phases has resets at arbitrarily large depths. That does not fix the phases the schedule reads.

**The one open statement.** On a strip, the least common period of each finite prefix is a power of two, and it doubles only just after a permanently white wheel, past every depth; both are in Lean. So one sufficient route is a lemma about those places: every white interval at which the period doubles contains a closed run in every phase. It holds wherever we have tested it: at every gap up to 30 for every power-of-two period, at every gap for power-of-two source periods up to 32, with no reset-free doubling at source period 64 below gap 74 except one undecided gap, and none at source period 128 up to gap 64. An exhaustive search over every source period finds no doubling at all at gaps 31 to 55, with machine-checked completeness at gaps 45, 50 and 51–55. Below source period 29, the six doublings that return to white within 100 wheels all come from a source with one black tick per period, and the only one with a reset-free tick has period 5, not a power of two. This lemma is what we are working on now.
