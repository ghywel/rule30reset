# A single gate bit recognizes the period-two trap

Watch the right neighbour of a black-first alternating column on white ticks. A window is *clean* if it contains none of `00000`, `11`, `101`, `10001` or `1001001`. From the **second white tick** on, 47 consecutive clean readings force the 16-cell window at the last reading into Bosdure’s 829-window trap. The previous gate bit can be discarded. A window beginning at the very first white tick requires 48 readings for the same five-word test. An entering right half has clean windows from six samples past entry, so the 47-sample test confirms by 52 intervals after entry. The finite row `0x7fffff80fc` attains 52, since a forbidden word beginning at offset 5 spoils every earlier complete window. The [cleanup proof](../WheelObserverCleanup.lean) supplies that row.

**Why one earlier sample matters.** Every one of the 512 nine-cell prefixes gives a black gate reading in its next five white samples. Of all clean 47-bit strings, just two contain none of I181’s nine forcing factors. Both begin with four zeros. Their preceding gate bit must therefore be black. Prepending that black produces a clean 48-bit string in either case. The established 48-bit theorem puts the common endpoint in the trap. All other clean strings contain a forcing factor, and closure carries membership to the endpoint. The [Lean proof](ObserverAfterFirst.lean) verifies the word coverage, both extensions and the physical implication, using the same nine physical cuts as the 48 theorem. This removes the extra width-23 cut used to classify 47 bits at an arbitrary start in I181.

**Two sharp window lengths.** The original finite inside and outside rows agree on all 47 readings, giving opposite membership at the last one. The outside block starts at sample 0 and the inside block at sample 10 after entry. Their suffixes of length 46 start at 1 and 11. Consequently 46 readings fail even when the observer knows one preceding sample existed. The same argument uses shorter suffixes for every positive length up to 46. This lower bound allows any decision rule on the supplied bits. A rule additionally given the exact age or the initial row would have different inputs. [Portable lower bounds and the five-sample lemma](PortableObserver.lean)

**Independent check.** I187 predicted an exterior realization of the exceptional word after ten earlier white samples with probability 0.50. Both the unrestricted first-white and finite black-first encodings refused it at start ages 10, 5, 2 and 1. At age 0 a black-first seed `0x146aaaaaaaaafe90d89896ad8659` produces it and ends outside with code 362. The literal Rule 30 table checks that seed and the interior control. The unrestricted age-one test covers every later age by shifting the driven row. An independently numbered OR/XOR cone split on the previous gate bit reproduced the conclusion. The prefix beginning with zero is impossible even without an endpoint restriction. The prefix beginning with one cannot end outside. Both LRAT checkers accepted all three critical certificates. ## Submission scope

Bosdure and collaborators supplied the 829-window spatial trap, closure and 3/5 gap law. The kit’s `Rule30GateTrapWords` excludes gap words after spatial entry, and `Rule30PeriodTwoOnset` gives short forbidden words from a specified onset. The observer adds the converse: a sufficiently long clean observation of only column 1 determines the hidden 16-cell membership at its endpoint. The certificate is uniform in the right half, its support, its eastern continuation and the start time subject to the explicit first-window exception. Entry is therefore equivalent to eventual detection. The remaining right-side prize question asks whether an aperiodic finite right half must ever enter.
