Drive a right half of Rule 30 with the alternating column, black first, and read column 1 at the white ticks: its gate. `Rule30GateTrap` has a closed set of 829 windows of sixteen cells, the trap, and limits the gate once the window is in it. Here is the converse, for every right half of any support: 48 consecutive readings that hold none of 00000, 11, 101, 10001 and 1001001 put the window at the last of them in the trap (`Rule30Submission.sound_48`), and 47 do if a white tick came before them (`sound_47_after_first`). From six samples after entry on every block is clean, so a right half enters the trap exactly when some block of 48 readings is clean (`enter_iff_some_clean`). Both lengths are least: no rule on fewer readings rejects every block that ends outside the trap and accepts every block that starts ten samples or more after entry (`no_short_classifier`, `no_short_classifier_after_first`). That uniform acceptance matters. Nine words of 20 to 38 readings each certify the trap (`factor_enters`), and one of them ends within 53 samples of every entry (`factor_after_entry`). The catalogue's `Rule30GateTrapWords` and `Rule30PeriodTwoOnset` restrict the gate and say nothing of the window behind it. The proof is one table of sets of times on spatial prefixes, as in `Rule30TrappedRuns`, on eleven time lines, checked by one walk in the kernel. Not claimed: that a finite right half enters the trap, or anything about eventual period two. `dichotomy_iff_clean` restates the first in the gate alone.
