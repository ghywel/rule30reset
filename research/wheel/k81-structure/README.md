# A 40-reading test for a departure from both K81 rhythms

Suppose the genuine single-black-cell evolution has a white centre at physical age A and the centre alternates through age 2A + 112, inclusive. Read its immediate right neighbour at the white ticks. Among the first ⌈A/2⌉ + 56 readings there are **40 consecutive bits that fit neither of K81’s two long-running rhythms, in any phase**. This is one visible obstruction to explaining the gate by changing between those two rhythms. No trap-entry hypothesis is needed. [Lean proof](WheelRhythmBreak.lean) · [Stock replay](stock.json)

The two rhythms repeat the pulse gaps `3555` and `355555`. One sample is two physical ticks. The first word therefore has period 18 samples, with pulses at residues 0, 3, 8 and 13. The wheel has period 28 samples, with pulses at 0, 3, 8, 13, 18 and 23. The test compares an observed string to the 46 possible starting phases.

**Why 40?** Thirty-nine samples distinguish all 46 phases. This is a direct finite check in Lean. Consecutive windows of length 40 share 39 samples, so whenever both fit, their phases must be consecutive phases of the same rhythm. Induction gives one template and phase on the entire interval if every length-40 window fits. At width 39 there is a genuine ambiguity. Align the two templates at a short gap from sample 0 to sample 3. They agree from −17 through 20. At both −18 and 21 the 18-cycle pulses and the wheel is white. Take the former rhythm’s value at −18 and the latter’s values through 21. Each length-39 subword of this 40-word fits and the whole word fits neither. That proves sharpness for recognition of the two periodic languages. This ambiguity occurs on an ordinary driven orbit. Start with every cell to the right of the black-first alternating wall white. At white samples 208 through 247 (physical ticks 417 through 495), its gate reads `1001000010000100001001000010000100001000`. The kit’s packed update, a scalar truth-table replay, and the direct simulator agree. Seed 0 was the first of the 256 registered rows to check. K81 also gives finite right rows with long pure stretches of each rhythm, and M106’s earlier witness supplies five complete wheel turns after 100 trapped samples. **Why a departure must occur.** If every 40-window fits, one candidate has physical period P = 36 or 56. The catalogue’s finite-window form of masked Jen compares ages A and A + P. Periodic centre readings through age 2(A + P) and matching white-time neighbour readings strictly before it would force the earlier singleton row to match the later row at its leftmost black cell. The centre hypothesis includes the endpoint, and the gate observations include every required earlier white tick for either period. Thus at least one 40-window fails both tests. A single misfit may straddle a change of rhythm or phase. The result does not require a third long-lived rhythm.

For indefinite alternation let s₀ = A and sₖ₊₁ = 2sₖ + 112 + (A mod 2). Each half-open physical interval [sₖ, sₖ₊₁) contains an entire rejected 40-word. At sₖ = 2ᵏ(A + c) − c, where c = 112 + (A mod 2), there have been at least k disjoint rejected words. In a reconstruction that assigns successive pieces to these templates, there must be either a faulty bit or an internal change of template or phase in each of those words. This rules out fewer than logarithmically many repairs to this two-rhythm model. It gives no positive-density bound, no age-independent cap for either rhythm on driven right halves, and no exclusion of eventual centre alternation.
